# The Formula Test and Who Fed the Stranger

**Status:** Public-narrative framework; individual cases require provenance and contextual verification  
**Created:** 2026-08-06  
**Purpose:** Use concrete mercy events to compare professed religion with lived fruit without reducing whole communities to viral anecdotes.

## The hook

> **Do not tell me only what your community believes. Show me what happens when a hungry stranger calls.**

Religious arguments often remain abstract:

- correct creed;
- correct scripture;
- correct history;
- correct identity;
- correct enemies.

A request for baby formula, food, work, shelter, or protection changes the level of analysis.

The question becomes:

> **Can belief reach a body in need?**

## The Formula Test

In late 2025, TikTok creator Nikalie Monroe conducted an informal, noncontrolled phone experiment in which she posed as the mother of a two-month-old who had gone without formula. She called numerous religious institutions. Some churches refused, redirected, or failed to provide immediate help; some churches did offer help; at least one Catholic church received broad praise; and the Islamic Center of Charlotte immediately asked where she lived and what formula she needed.

The experiment was deceptive and methodologically weak as social science:

- the baby was fictional;
- calls were not fully standardized;
- phone staff may not have known institutional resources;
- response capacity varied;
- edited short videos cannot establish complete institutional behavior;
- some churches had external aid programs that were not communicated during the call;
- viral selection may amplify dramatic failures.

Yet the event remains a legitimate **interface audit**.

It exposed a real institutional question:

> When a person presents an urgent need, can the public-facing system move from procedure to care before the emergency deepens?

## Strongest interpretation

The lesson is not:

> Mosques always help; churches never help.

The lesson is:

> **Mercy must be operational before the call arrives.**

An institution should not depend on whether one receptionist happens to know the correct number.

A functioning mercy system needs:

- a small emergency fund;
- verified local resource routing;
- clear authority for urgent purchases;
- after-hours escalation;
- privacy protection;
- human follow-up;
- fraud-aware compassion without default suspicion;
- a way to learn from failed calls.

## Who Fed the Stranger?

This narrative series examines moments in which the group portrayed as threatening becomes the helper, while the group publicly associated with virtue fails—or the reverse.

The purpose is not humiliation.

It is moral surprise followed by institutional learning.

### Story architecture

1. **Expectation** — What the audience has been taught about the group.
2. **Need** — A concrete vulnerable person, not an ideological abstraction.
3. **Response** — What each institution or community actually does.
4. **Fruit** — Timeliness, dignity, sacrifice, and effectiveness.
5. **Context** — What the viral clip leaves out.
6. **Mirror** — Turn the test inward on every community.
7. **Mechanism** — Identify why help did or did not arrive.
8. **Renewal** — Build a system that reproduces the good fruit.

## Candidate Episode 1 — The Formula Test

Opening:

> A woman called religious institutions and said her two-month-old had not eaten.

Do not begin with the mosque result. Let the audience experience the uncertainty.

Reveal:

> The first institution to respond simply asked where she was and what the baby needed.

Then reveal it was a mosque.

Immediately add the truth guardrail:

> Some churches also helped. The experiment was not controlled. But that does not erase the question it exposed.

Close:

> **Theology became visible in the time between the request and the answer.**

## Candidate Episode 2 — The Pickens County Witness

Founder-provided testimony describes a formerly homeless man saying Muslims first gave him work, two local people helped shelter him, and Latino community members later employed him in a Georgia community he experienced as publicly Christian but practically indifferent.

Public use remains blocked until the original video and context are secured.

Narrative line:

> **The people he had been taught to misunderstand became the people who saw him.**

## Candidate Episode 3 — The Gaza Call

On March 17, 2026, a caller on *Chris Fabry Live* raised Gaza and the Christian response. Michael Rydelnik answered by describing the genocide allegation as propaganda and linked listeners to his article arguing Israel was not committing genocide.

The episode should not be presented as proof that all Christians deny Palestinian suffering.

It is a case study in **sacred narrative defense**:

- evidence threatening a theological-political framework is redescribed as propaganda;
- condemnation of Hamas becomes the entry condition for discussing Palestinian civilians;
- population-growth arguments are used to dismiss genocide claims despite the legal definition not requiring population decline;
- Christian Palestinian testimony is absent;
- the modern state receives interpretive protection from the tradition that should be judging power.

Counterweight:

Include Palestinian Christians, the Patriarchs and Heads of Churches in Jerusalem, the World Council of Churches, churches opposing Christian Zionism, and Christians whose faith demands solidarity with Palestinians.

## The Jesus-native test

The Christian audience does not need to accept Islamic theology to understand the critique.

Use Jesus’s own standards:

- the tree known by fruit;
- the Good Samaritan;
- the least of these;
- saying “Lord, Lord” versus doing God’s will;
- the danger of serving wealth;
- mercy over public religious performance.

> **Let Jesus judge the Christianity practiced in his name.**

## The Muslim mirror

Muslims may not use good mosque stories as moral trophies.

Ask:

- Would every mosque answer as well?
- Can women, converts, refugees, disabled people, debtors, and dissenters access help with dignity?
- Is zakat routed effectively?
- Are requests buried under committees and social judgment?
- Does the mosque help non-Muslims?
- Can mercy survive sect, race, nationality, and reputation?

> **The point is not to win the anecdote. It is to reproduce the fruit.**
