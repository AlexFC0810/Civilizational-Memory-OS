# The Open Social Commons

## A Public Manifesto for Cognitive Sovereignty

Our identities, relationships, communities, memories, and public conversations should not belong to a handful of private platforms.

Today, leaving a social network can mean losing your audience. A community can spend years building trust, archives, culture, and relationships—then discover that one owner, company sale, algorithm change, policy shift, or account suspension can disrupt everything.

This is not genuine digital freedom.

We believe:

- people should be able to keep their identity when they change applications;
- communities should be able to preserve their relationships and archives;
- users should be able to choose feeds, moderation systems, and providers;
- funding, governance, and recommendation systems should be inspectable;
- no founder, billionaire, corporation, government, or donor should be able to capture the whole public conversation;
- successful open infrastructure should remain forkable, replaceable, and accountable.

The alternatives are not imaginary. Builders across the world have already created open protocols, federated networks, portable identity systems, community-governance tools, collective-intelligence platforms, transparent funding systems, and cooperative ownership models.

The missing layer is coordination.

People need to see the alternatives, understand the tradeoffs, trust the evaluation, find the right path, move with their communities, and direct money and skills toward what remains unfinished.

That is the purpose of the Open Social Commons.

It will serve as a:

- clarity map;
- solutions atlas;
- migration office;
- public evaluation layer;
- contributor switchboard;
- gap registry;
- transparent treasury;
- and shared memory for the open social internet.

We will not replace one private empire with another.

We will build infrastructure through which no throne is necessary.

## The First Action

# Move One Community

Do not merely abandon a platform alone.

Bring one group, audience, congregation, team, family, institution, or online community toward infrastructure that it can understand, leave, govern, and preserve.

Start gradually:

1. map your dependencies;
2. claim an open identity;
3. mirror one channel or workflow;
4. move one complete community;
5. help maintain or improve the commons.

You do not need to decentralize everything overnight.

You need to begin eliminating fatal dependencies.

## The Commitment

We will name actors, institutions, incentives, and mechanisms precisely.

We will not answer dehumanization with counter-dehumanization.

We will not allow sponsorship to purchase favorable evaluation.

We will publish limitations and failures alongside successes.

We will design the Commons to survive its founders.

We will measure communities empowered—not attention accumulated at the center.

> **Attention must terminate in infrastructure.**

> **Build the switchboard, not the empire.**

> **A civilization becomes free not when it has no centers of coordination, but when its centers remain accountable, replaceable, and impossible to own.**
