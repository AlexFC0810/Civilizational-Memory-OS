## Mission

Build the coordination, evaluation, migration, and funding layer that helps communities move from privately captive digital systems toward open, portable, governable infrastructure.

> **We do not mainly lack alternatives. We lack a compiler.**

> **Build the switchboard, not the empire.**

## The Conversion Problem

The project exists to convert moral and public concern into organized capability:

```text
Concern
→ clarity
→ trust
→ commitment
→ contribution
→ coordinated work
→ visible success
→ wider adoption
```

The website is not the final product. It is the front door and control surface for a living network of:

- builders;
- evaluators;
- migration specialists;
- creators;
- community operators;
- funders;
- governance and legal experts;
- researchers;
- and pilot communities.

The true scarce resource is **trusted direction**: knowing which systems are serious, which tradeoffs matter, which path fits a community, and what action will produce real movement.

## Public Constitutional Layer

The coalition does not require identical politics. It requires agreement on a narrow constitutional layer:

- identity should be portable;
- communities should retain exit rights;
- algorithms and moderation should be meaningfully choosable;
- power, funding, and governance should be inspectable;
- no single owner, founder, donor, company, or government should be able to capture the whole network;
- infrastructure should remain forkable, exportable, and replaceable.

## Universal CTA

# Move One Community

> **Do not merely leave a platform. Bring one community with you.**

The atomic unit of migration is the community, not the isolated individual. An individual who leaves often enters an empty room. A community that moves creates a viable node.

The participation ladder is:

1. **Map** — understand current dependencies.
2. **Claim** — create a portable or open identity.
3. **Mirror** — establish an open-system presence without abandoning the old platform.
4. **Move** — bring one group, audience, team, institution, or community.
5. **Maintain** — host, moderate, fund, document, or govern.
6. **Build** — improve the underlying infrastructure or fill a documented gap.

## Minimum Viable Digital Sovereignty

A community has minimum viable digital sovereignty when it can:

1. export its data;
2. preserve member relationships;
3. change providers without disappearing;
4. communicate outside one platform;
5. retain an independent archive;
6. understand who governs its tools;
7. survive the failure, capture, or sale of one vendor.

> **You do not need to decentralize everything. You need to eliminate fatal dependencies.**

### 1. Clarity Map

Explain where control currently resides across identity, data, social graph, discovery, ranking, moderation, payments, hosting, governance, and funding.

### 2. Solutions Atlas

Create structured, evidence-aware profiles of serious open-social, communications, governance, collective-intelligence, funding, identity, moderation, and hosting projects.

### 3. Open Infrastructure Test

### 4. Migration Wizard and Playbooks

See [`MOVE_ONE_COMMUNITY_PLAYBOOK.md`](MOVE_ONE_COMMUNITY_PLAYBOOK.md).

### 5. Commons Gap Map

Document missing public infrastructure and convert each gap into a specification, bounty, funding target, contributor route, pilot, and measurable completion condition.

See [`COMMONS_GAP_MAP_TEMPLATE.md`](COMMONS_GAP_MAP_TEMPLATE.md).

### 6. Coordination and Assembly

Connect builders, funders, creators, institutions, researchers, and communities while keeping durable work public, documented, and forkable.

### 7. Transparent Treasury

Show money received, donor concentration, spending, milestones, responsible parties, outputs, and unresolved risks.

### 8. Progress Ledger

Track movement into open infrastructure rather than vanity traffic to the central website.

Core measures include:

- communities mapped, mirrored, and moved;
- users retained after 30 and 90 days;
- portable identities created;
- independent servers and hosts established;
- open-source contributions;
- migration costs and time;
- documented gaps closed;
- projects funded;
- and communities that can survive a vendor failure.

```text
This happened
→ here is the governing mechanism
→ here is the alternative
→ here is the project solving it
→ here is what it needs
→ here is your next action
```

> **Attention must terminate in infrastructure.**

## Founding Role

The founding synthesizer functions as:

- **cartographer** — map the solution space;
- **evaluator** — distinguish genuine openness from branding;
- **translator** — make architecture intelligible to normal people;
- **convener** — connect separated communities of expertise;
- **migration architect** — design adoption pathways;
- **capital router** — direct funding and contributors toward high-leverage gaps;
- **narrative supercharger** — make open infrastructure culturally compelling.

The founder should remain a high-signal catalyst, not the permanent bottleneck or benevolent owner of decentralization.

## Anti-Capture Rule

The Commons must embody its doctrine. It should be:

- open source;
- forkable;
- exportable;
- mirrored;
- transparently funded;
- protocol-neutral;
- governed by a public constitution;
- and able to survive the absence of any individual founder.

> **A civilization becomes free not when it has no centers of coordination, but when its centers remain accountable, replaceable, and impossible to own.**
