# Commons Gap Map Template

## Purpose

The Solutions Atlas shows what exists.

The Commons Gap Map shows what is missing and converts each missing piece into coordinated, fundable work.

> **The objective is not merely to curate solutions. It is to create a market for missing public goods.**

## Gap Record

### 1. Gap Title

A specific, plain-language name.

### 2. User Problem

Describe the human problem before the technical abstraction.

- Who experiences it?
- What are they currently unable to do?
- What does the failure cost in time, money, safety, trust, audience, or autonomy?

### 3. Affected Communities

Identify the primary groups:

- creators;
- civic organizations;
- religious communities;
- families;
- journalists;
- developers;
- schools;
- local governments;
- vulnerable users;
- cooperatives;
- other.

### 4. Current Workarounds

Document how people cope today and why those methods are insufficient.

### 5. Existing Partial Solutions

List relevant projects, standards, integrations, research, and prototypes.

For each, state:

- what it solves;
- what remains missing;
- maturity;
- interoperability;
- ownership and governance;
- known limitations.

### 6. Missing Component

Define the exact missing capability.

Examples:

- cross-protocol identity;
- reliable community migration tooling;
- accessible account recovery;
- portable moderation preferences;
- independent archives;
- algorithm marketplaces;
- protocol bridges;
- creator monetization without audience captivity;
- simple self-hosting;
- multilingual onboarding;
- cooperative legal templates;
- non-surveillance business models.

### 7. Why the Market Has Not Solved It

Possible causes:

- weak direct monetization;
- coordination failure;
- fragmented standards;
- high integration cost;
- regulatory uncertainty;
- network effects;
- public-good free-rider problem;
- insufficient visibility;
- lack of pilot demand;
- incumbent obstruction;
- technical difficulty.

### 8. Public-Interest Specification

Define:

- required functionality;
- interoperability requirements;
- data formats;
- privacy and safety requirements;
- accessibility requirements;
- governance expectations;
- licensing expectations;
- anti-capture constraints;
- documentation requirements.

Avoid overspecifying implementation before builder input, but make the public-interest outcome testable.

### 9. Completion Conditions

A gap is not closed because a prototype exists.

State measurable completion conditions such as:

- works across named systems;
- tested by a minimum number of communities;
- preserves required data and relationships;
- passes recovery and security tests;
- usable by nontechnical administrators;
- documented and open source;
- independent audit completed;
- 30- and 90-day pilot retention achieved.

### 10. Pilot Community

Identify a real community willing to test the solution.

Record:

- use case;
- size;
- constraints;
- responsible contact;
- success measures;
- consent and safety requirements.

### 11. Contributors Needed

- protocol engineer;
- application engineer;
- designer;
- researcher;
- technical writer;
- translator;
- security reviewer;
- legal expert;
- community operator;
- product manager;
- funder;
- evaluator.

### 12. Funding Target

Record:

- discovery budget;
- prototype budget;
- pilot budget;
- maintenance budget;
- audit budget;
- estimated ongoing cost;
- funding source and concentration risk.

### 13. Governance and Ownership

Specify how the resulting public good will remain:

- accessible;
- maintainable;
- forkable;
- transparently governed;
- resistant to private capture;
- transferable if the original team disappears.

### 14. Risks

Include:

- privacy;
- abuse;
- centralization;
- legal exposure;
- security;
- maintenance burden;
- protocol fragmentation;
- adoption failure;
- dependency on one vendor or donor.

### 15. Status

Use one of:

- observed;
- researching;
- specification open;
- seeking pilot;
- seeking contributors;
- seeking funding;
- building;
- pilot active;
- validating;
- closed;
- paused;
- rejected with rationale.

### 16. Evidence and Decision Log

Preserve:

- source links;
- user interviews;
- pilot data;
- decisions;
- rejected approaches;
- conflicts of interest;
- revision history.

## Prioritization Test

Prioritize gaps by:

1. severity of user harm;
2. number of communities affected;
3. importance to migration or sovereignty;
4. tractability;
5. availability of pilot demand;
6. reuse across protocols and communities;
7. neglectedness;
8. risk of private capture if left unsolved;
9. ability to unlock further ecosystem growth.

## Final Routing Question

> **What is the smallest public good that, if built now, would unlock the greatest amount of stranded community energy?**
