## Decision: the question beneath the historical episodes

> **When one person or institution has power over a being who cannot resist, what makes that power answerable to law, mercy, truth and correction?**

This extends the accepted framework in `canon/CIVILIZATIONAL_NARRATIVE_OPERATING_SYSTEM.md` ("power severed from truth, mercy and accountability"), `canon/FRUIT_TEST_AND_MORAL_ARCHITECTURE.md`, `canon/FRUIT_STACK_AND_COMPARATIVE_HISTORY_METHOD.md`, `canon/ANIMAL_CIVILIZATION_BENCHMARK.md`, and the working `research-programs/MORAL_PROGRESS_IS_NOT_AUTOMATIC_THESIS_2026-11-12.md`. **It is a focused comparative research test, not another civilizational operating system.**

**The distinct test:** knowledge, wealth, architecture and power are capacities; law and institutions can set constraints; documented practice reveals who actually benefited. A source can expose terrible individual conduct without proving a whole culture's permanent character. An admirable rule can exist without universal compliance. Neither asymmetry should erase the evidence.

## Case PWR-01 — Nero, Sporus and the vulnerability of the powerless

**Period:** Rome under Nero (r. 54–68 CE); Sporus's reported death **after Nero**, under Vitellius in 69 CE.

**Primary literary witnesses:**
- Suetonius, `Life of Nero` 28: Nero castrated Sporus, staged a marriage and displayed him as his wife. https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Suetonius/12Caesars/Nero%2A.html
- Cassius Dio, `Roman History` 62.28: independently repeats the Sporus event with an explanation linked to resemblance to Poppaea. https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/62%2A.html
- Cassius Dio 64.10: under **Vitellius**, Sporus reportedly killed himself rather than endure a staged public sexual violation. https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/64%2A.html

**Source criticism:**
- The sources are later imperial authors, not objective modern court depositions. Opponents' political and sexual misconduct allegations were a literary convention; separate convergence, rumor and inferred motivation.
- David Woods, "Nero and Sporus", *Latomus* 68(1) (2009), pp.73–82 offers a rival motive involving humiliation of a potential dynastic rival, rather than primarily desire or love: https://cora.ucc.ie/handle/10468/33
- No primary witness in this packet establishes that Nero "passed Sporus around to other men" as a general practice. Don't recite that embellishment without verification; the later threat of public humiliation is separately reported by Dio.
- **Chronology:** Nero was a **first-century** ruler, centuries before Islam began in the seventh century. Comparing his court's scandal directly with Abbasid law is **not same-century comparability**. Label it an **across-era moral problem / case study in power**, not the baseline of Roman civilization at Islam's emergence.
- A contemporary taxi-driver story is **not evidence that contemporary Italians are unchanged Romans**, nor of Roman-wide societal character; don't make such ethnic/national essentialist inferences.

**Research questions:** What Roman legal restraints, elite power structures, anti-castration laws and political mechanisms existed? Were other Roman elites/subjects critical of Nero? What do juristic and material records show of power and slavery across imperial society? Find a matching later Roman/Byzantine comparator when asking about seventh-century Islam.

**Safe interim narrative:** "Ancient writers report Nero's coercive treatment of Sporus; they also report Sporus's tragic fate after Nero's death. The story raises a larger question about how systems restrain the powerful, but it cannot represent all Romans or a seventh-century contemporaneous comparison."

**UNSAFE:** "All Romans were savages"; "the suicide occurred while Nero was alive"; "the podcast verifies the story"; "one emperor proves no Roman women could own property"; imagined forced-sex scenes shown as archive.

## Case PWR-02 — Women's rights: from actual scriptures to court records, not slogans

**Islamic legal layers:**
- Qur'an 4:7 assigns women inheritance shares; Qur'an 4:11 gives different quantified shares in defined family arrangements. https://quran.com/an-nisa/7/translations/en ; https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=4&verse=11
- Mariam Sheibani, "Marriage, Divorce, and Inheritance in Classical Islamic Law and Premodern Practice," *Oxford Handbook of Islam and Women*, describes variation across five major schools and the gap between formal rules and local practice: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/55356/chapter-abstract/430533035
- Amira Sonbol, "Women in Shari'ah Courts: A Historical and Methodological Discussion", *Fordham International Law Journal* 27(1) (2003): women litigated, negotiated contracts and employed divorce mechanisms in premodern Islamic courts, with regional variation: https://ir.lawnet.fordham.edu/ilj/vol27/iss1/9/
- Leslie Peirce, *Morality Tales: Law and Gender in the Ottoman Court of Aintab*, chapter "Women, Property, and the Court," documents women navigating ownership and familial constraints: https://doi.org/10.1525/california/9780520228900.003.0007
- Fatma Müge Göçek and Marc David Baer, "Social Boundaries of Ottoman Women's Experience in Eighteenth-Century Galata Court Records": 703 cases sampled in 1729/1769/1789, ~28% involving women; includes property, inheritance and legal claims. https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/gocek/wp-content/uploads/sites/352/2016/01/GocekMuge.bc_.Womens-Experience-in-Ottoman-Society-1996.pdf
- Caveat: male unilateral divorce and gender-asymmetric shares/other norms were real; women did not hold an identical legal status to men in every area. Yossef Rapoport, *Marriage, Money and Divorce in Medieval Islamic Society* (Cambridge, 2005): https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/marriage-money-and-divorce-in-medieval-islamic-society/35A5E65936B2522D05DB7ED7C3149D77/listing

**Critical Roman counterevidence** (to avoid an evidence-poor "only Islam allowed this"):
- Andrew M. Riggsby, *Roman Law and the Legal World of the Romans*, chapter "Women and Property": free women could own/inherit property, make contracts, sue and be sued, with specified exclusions, family-power limits and guardianship: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/roman-law-and-the-legal-world-of-the-romans/women-and-property/3228BB58B76BE5BC4167ADED678763AF
- Edward E. Cohen, *Roman Inequality: Affluent Slaves, Businesswomen, Legal Fictions* (Oxford, 2023), chapter "Businesswomen": Roman women participated substantially in commerce and property markets: https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780197687345.003.0005
- *Journal of Roman Studies*, "The End of Tutela Mulierum" (2024) documents relative Roman female property independence despite guardianship constraints; compare cities, status, centuries: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-roman-studies/article/end-of-tutela-mulierum/893480017AD050374B0ABDBD218050AB

**Stronger comparison question:** Across *fixed* centuries/cities/classes, what could a widow or merchant woman own, inherit, alienate, litigate, fund and keep from her husband? What recourse did she have against male relatives or judges? Compare primary court records with primary court records and avoid equating the written law with universally favorable real outcomes.

**Safe interim narrative:** "Classical Islamic law guaranteed specified female inheritance and property entitlements, and many women demonstrably asserted them in Ottoman courts. Imperial Roman law also allowed women substantial private property and business rights. The strongest comparison lies in the scope, enforcement and beneficiaries of their respective legal systems—not in saying one side had no rights."

**Potential episode object:** One surviving Galata legal entry with a named woman, date and claimed property, faithfully transcribed. **Not yet identified**; production must not invent a representative litigant.

## Case PWR-03 — Jerusalem twice: massacre, negotiated surrender and the limits of mercy

**Nature:** **Time-separated event comparison**, not same-year or identical strategic circumstances.  
**Event A:** The First Crusade captured Jerusalem **15 July 1099**; the conquerors massacred large numbers of non-Christian inhabitants. Specialist contextualization: https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190937805.003.0012 ; recent historiographical article on the special/exemplary status of 1099 and siege norms: https://doi.org/10.1080/14765276.2025.2464743
**Event B:** Saladin's forces took Jerusalem after a negotiated surrender on **2 October 1187**. Many residents departed under ransom arrangements; those unable to secure ransom were at risk of enslavement. Historiography on Ernoul's account and the politically negotiated terms: https://doi.org/10.1080/14765276.2024.2359668 ; John D. Hosler, *Jerusalem Falls: Seven Centuries of War and Peace* (Yale University Press, 2022), https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Hosler-John-D.-Jerusalem-Falls_-Seven-Centuries-of-War-and-Peace-Yale-University-Press-2022.pdf

**Why powerful:** Real, documented difference in *how two conquering armies handled an exceptionally vulnerable population*. The outcome of 1187 cannot be described as an unconditional release or modern equality: ransom and enslavement remain part of the story. 1099 cannot simply be the "normal Christian method" for all sieges: specialists describe broader medieval siege customs and diplomatic practices. The comparison is a **specific pair of captures**, not proof of every Christian or Muslim army's character.

**Institutional question:** How did command decisions, diplomatic surrender, legal norms, religious interpretations, economics and strategic incentives contribute to these outcomes? Which rules were actually enforceable? What happened to the unredeemed and to minority communities?

**Primary-source gap:** Retrieve Arabic/Latin narratives (Ibn al-Athir, Baha ad-Din ibn Shaddad, Ernoul, Fulcher/other near-contemporary accounts) with translation and manuscript-context review; register disputed casualty and ransom estimates with their own claims and grades before quoting numbers.

**Safe interim narrative:** "1099 and 1187 are not identical sieges. But their different treatment of Jerusalem's inhabitants offers a rare window into conquest, restraint, bargaining, massacre and enslavement."

**Do not publish:** "Saladin freed every civilian without payment"; "medieval Christians always massacred"; "all Muslim armies obeyed laws of war"; "all medieval armies lacked norms."

## Case PWR-04 — The caliph and the hungry children: ideal of entrusted power

**Story in circulation:** A report about Caliph ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, a mother with hungry children, and his carrying flour and preparing food. Popular retelling: https://www.islamweb.net/en/women/article/143905/a-glimpse-at-the-life-of-%E2%80%98umar-ibn-al-khattaab-%E2%80%93-iii

**Important:** The link is a devotional recounting, not a completed audit of earliest source, isnād, date, chain, context or reliable historical consensus. **Status: STORY REQUIRES PRIMARY-REPORT PROVENANCE.** This is a potentially powerful exemplary narrative but not yet the basis for causal claims about all caliphs or populations. Distinguish ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb from later ruler ʿUmar II / ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, whose pious-anecdote corpus also requires source grading.

**Research objective:** Locate earliest Arabic report, narrator/ascription, chronology and scholarly treatment. Identify court/treasury/relief policy and administrative accountability **independent** of anecdote, rather than equating a pious story with statistically successful welfare.

**Safer public frame:** "A widely circulated tradition portrays a caliph personally carrying food for hungry children; whether every detail of this story is historically secure must be established separately from the model of responsible leadership it expresses."

## Case PWR-05 — The modern psychology of contact, dominance, and empathy

**Working thesis:** Humans are an evolved social primate species with tendencies toward coalition, status, aggression, empathy, reciprocity and cooperation. Human behavior is plastic, context-sensitive and governed partly by norms and institutions; "we are territorial apes" is an expressive model, **not a license to declare all people naturally cruel or incapable of moral restraint**. Humans are apes in cladistic terms; we did not evolve from any living modern ape species.

**Handshake/oxytocin claim:** It is too strong to state as a universal scientific fact that every skin-to-skin handshake releases oxytocin. Experimental research on affectionate/partner touch and social-context dependence is narrower than such a claim: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10168694/ . A 2024 meta-analysis of 51 pooled studies found inconsistent causal oxytocin changes across human social interactions: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11077008/ . Human social touch does have documented associations with wellbeing and context-dependent responses: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-023-00217-5
**Research question:** What behavioral change does actual touch cause, for whom and under what consent/cultural conditions? Do not derive an obligatory handshake ritual or interpersonal manipulation script from thin hormone claims.

**Religious explanatory layer:** A believer may describe a stable divine moral framework as a technology for disciplining appetite and limiting domination. That is a valuable *worldview interpretation*. Empirical historical inquiry can test *whether particular communities translated norms into institutions and outcomes*, but cannot establish divine origin scientifically. Human motives are multicausal: law, trade, plural expertise, coercion, patronage, social trust and political incentives all matter.

## Revision of the cognitive model

**From:** "A cruel emperor proves Roman degeneracy; a merciful ruler proves Islamic superiority."

**To:** "Both civilizations contain moral texts, institutions, exemplary and abusive rulers. Investigate what conditions shaped the distribution of power, whether the powerless had legal recourse, and how individual good or evil scaled into repeatable institutions." It is possible to demonstrate a particularly important Islamic legal innovation without falsely erasing Roman or other achievements.

### Five questions for ANY future story
1. Who has power and who cannot resist? Name both rather than a whole population.
2. Is the story supported by contemporary records, retrospective chronicles, moralizing literature, or later retellings? How might its genre distort it?
3. Which norm, sanction, legal remedy, funding stream or accountability institution could have changed the outcome?
4. What did *comparable* contemporaries actually do, and which inconvenient successes or failures must we also show?
5. What evidence would narrow or reverse the story's suggested conclusion, and what can today's institution learn from it?
