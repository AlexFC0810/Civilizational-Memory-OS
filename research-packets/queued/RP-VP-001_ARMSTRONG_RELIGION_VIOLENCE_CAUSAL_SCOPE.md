## 1. Exact question

Is the following proposition historically and causally defensible across relevant modern cases?

> Separating religious institutions from the state is not, by itself, sufficient to eliminate war or mass political violence.

This packet does **not** ask whether religion is generally peaceful or violent, whether secularism is good or bad, or whether religious ideas ever have independent causal force.

## 2. Why it matters

Public argument often collapses several different claims:

1. religious establishment can contribute to coercion;
2. institutional secularization can reduce some establishment harms;
3. secularization is sufficient for peace;
4. religion is the primary cause of war;
5. secular ideologies are the primary cause of modern violence.

Only the third proposition is being tested here. A durable verdict would strengthen the Civilizational Intelligence framework by preventing both religious apologetics and secular monocausal triumphalism.

## 3. Proposed knowledge object

### Strongest honest formulation

> Separating religious institutions from the state is not, by itself, sufficient to eliminate war or mass political violence; nationalism, empire, race, class, territory, security institutions, revolutionary ideology, and state capacity can organize violence through nonreligious or mixed legitimating systems.

### Tempting stronger formulations prohibited

- `Religion does not cause violence.`
- `All wars blamed on religion are really political.`
- `Secularism causes mass violence.`
- `Religious societies are more peaceful than secular societies.`
- `Religious language is never independently causal.`

## 8. Scope lock

- **Time period:** primarily modern state era; earlier cases only for category genealogy
- **Geography:** cross-regional, selected through explicit case criteria
- **Institution type:** states, armed movements, empires, and religious establishments
- **Definitions:**
  - `institutional secularization` must be separated from atheism, declining belief, religious freedom, anticlericalism, and state control of religion;
  - `mass political violence` must be defined by the dataset or historical literature used;
  - `sufficient` means the condition reliably produces the outcome without additional necessary conditions.
- **Does not establish:** that secularization has no peace-promoting effects, that religion is never causal, or that all political systems are morally equivalent.

## 9. Comparative-frame audit

### Required comparisons

- secular ideal vs religious ideal;
- secular-state practice vs religious-state practice;
- matched periods and state capacities where possible;
- similar conflict types and material constraints;
- trajectory vs trajectory rather than selected summit vs selected failure.

### Endpoint-selection warning

Do not compare:

- the most compassionate religious texts with twentieth-century secular atrocities;
- the worst confessional wars with the best-performing contemporary secular democracies;
- premodern low-capacity violence totals with industrial-state totals without population, technology, and state-capacity controls.

### Rival comparison

A strong rival formulation may be:

> Liberal secular institutions are not sufficient for peace but can reduce specific establishment, sectarian, and conscience-related harms when combined with accountable government, pluralism, prosperity, and legal restraint.

The final verdict must be compatible with that possibility if evidence supports it.

## 10. Causal integrity

- **Proposed cause being rejected as sufficient:** separation of religious institutions from state power
- **Possible mediators:** pluralism, conscience protections, legitimacy, institutional restraint
- **Confounders:** wealth, democracy, state capacity, education, security environment, colonial history, ethnic composition
- **Alternative causes of violence:** nationalism, territorial dispute, empire, racial hierarchy, revolutionary ideology, resource competition, security dilemma
- **Reverse causation:** peaceful and stable societies may be more able to sustain pluralistic secular arrangements
- **Selection problems:** coding violence as religious whenever religious actors or vocabulary are present
- **Independent causal weight unresolved:** doctrines, sacred authority, religious networks, and eschatological beliefs may change actor preferences in particular cases

## 11. Strongest supporting case

Modern history supplies abundant examples of war and mass violence under states and movements that were institutionally secular, anticlerical, ethnonationalist, revolutionary, imperial, or mixed. This defeats the strong claim that political separation of religion and state is sufficient for peace. It also shows that human domination can be legitimated through nation, race, class, security, civilization, revolution, or progress.

## 12. Strongest opposing case

Institutional secularization may still reduce important mechanisms of violence: clerical coercion, enforced orthodoxy, persecution of dissenters, legal inequality by confession, and zero-sum struggles over sacred state authority. Some secular constitutional orders have produced durable religious peace precisely by constraining establishments and protecting pluralism. The failure of secularization to guarantee peace does not show it lacks causal value.

## 13. Breaker evidence

This proposition would be narrowed or reclassified if:

- credible cross-national evidence showed that institutional secularization, independently of major confounders, was close to sufficient for preventing the relevant classes of mass political violence;
- allegedly secular cases proved substantially governed by religious institutions under the packet's locked definition;
- the claim relied on redefining any nonreligious violence as “secular violence” without a causal mechanism;
- the evidence only established that secular states have committed violence, which is necessary but not sufficient for a causal model.

## 14. Provisional verdict

- **Status:** probable as a narrow insufficiency claim
- **Confidence:** 75%
- **Reasoning:** counterexamples are strong enough to reject secularization as a standalone guarantee of peace, but the packet has not yet measured what specific harms particular secular arrangements reduce or resolved causal confounding.
- **Unknown:** effect size across forms of secularism and types of violence
- **Next action:** source closure plus bounded comparative case matrix

This confidence is a research triage estimate, not a canon verdict.
