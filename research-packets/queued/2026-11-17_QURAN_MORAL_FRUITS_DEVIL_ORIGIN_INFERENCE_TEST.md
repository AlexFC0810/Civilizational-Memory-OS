## 3. Distinguish three questions that a popular video tends to collapse
**T1 — What does the text actually say?** A textual/philological question resolvable with the Arabic Qur'an, translations and tafsir, provided passages are read with context and difficult texts not omitted.
**T2 — What kind of person and society does the text aim to form?** A theological/normative interpretation, with measurable hypotheses about conscience, honesty, generosity, justice, power, and the *actual institutions and harms* arising under different interpretations.
**T3 — What is the revelation's ultimate origin?** A metaphysical/theological judgment not deductively proven by T1/T2 alone; Muslims affirm revelation from Allah, others offer differing explanations. Moral consonance is an **argument about plausibility within a theological worldview**, not a laboratory test or a logically necessary demonstration.

**Logical tension to preserve:** The argument “The Qur'an teaches against Satan's vices, therefore Satan could not author it” needs an additional premise that a deceptive being could *never* communicate true virtues for misleading reasons. That premise isn't logically forced. Alternatives include human moral composition and differing religious interpretations. A confident argument should acknowledge this, not falsely claim a formal proof.

**Strong fair steelman:** Some religious critics claim deceptive powers can mix true moral teachings with false doctrine to mislead; some secular readers may agree with moral teachings without attributing them to the divine. Serious discussion would then examine the text's theological claims, internal coherence, hard passages, transmission history and actual practice, not repeat an impossible binary.

**Strong supporter reconstruction (not a proof):** In a text that explicitly commands refuge against Satan, repeatedly demands justice and self-scrutiny, and condemns corruption, blanket labels of “evil” carry an evidentiary burden. A critic should identify the objectionable text, its actual interpretation, context, strongest internal moral principles and the empirical impact of its implementation. The natural invitation is “read the text and form your own judgment,” not “we have logically ruled out every alternative source.”

## 5. Five high-leverage ethical/epistemic research questions
1. **Would we apply the same “judge by fruits” argument if a religious text outside Islam also teaches self-restraint, charity and justice?** If not, our test is biased.
2. **Can a text mandate virtue and still permit actions we judge oppressive today?** How do informed Muslim and non-Muslim scholars interpret the hardest passages (e.g., 9:29, 4:34) without euphemism?
3. **What evidence would distinguish an inspiring moral exhortation from causally effective social accountability?** Named rules, budgets, audited enforcement and actual beneficiaries.
4. **What is the strongest *specific* evidence any opponent offers for calling the Qur'an devilish, and what theological premises does it require?** Do not invent a generic strawman or assign it to all Christians.
5. **What should be the outcome for a viewer who reads and still disagrees?** Respect for freedom of conscience and substantially greater text literacy can be good outcomes without conversion.
