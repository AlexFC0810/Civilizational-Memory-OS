# CASE — Fāṭima's water-rights lawsuit (Khurāsān, 26 May 1212)

**Status:** PRIMARY-DOCUMENT-BASED SOURCE CANDIDATE / evidence reconstructed from published *edition and translation*; **not yet CMOS source-closed or publish-cleared.**  
**Date researched:** 2026-11-13. **Owner:** CMOS legal-history / women-and-law research seat, [issue #114](https://github.com/AlexFC0810/Civilizational-Memory-OS/issues/114).  
**Reason discovered:** Move the debate about historical Islamic women's rights from sweeping slogans to a **named woman's actual surviving court proceeding**, with the outcome and source-limits intact.  
**Connected question:** *When human beings acquire power over someone who cannot resist them, what determines whether they exploit that power or restrain themselves?* More narrowly: **could a woman bring a property claim against a man, and how did the judge decide it?**

## Primary historical object (unusually strong provenance)

- **Object:** `Khalili doc.51`, Nasser D. Khalili Collection of Islamic Art, London; original New Persian document written in Perso-Arabic script. **Recto** 33 lines + 5 witness clauses, **verso** 4 witness clauses. Paper measuring **56.5 × 11.2 cm**.
- **Date:** 15 Dhū l-Ḥijja 608 AH / **26 May 1212 CE**.
- **Region:** Islamic Khurāsān, in the region of present-day Afghanistan. The exact settlement name in the original is **uncertain**; do not invent a specific village, modern city or nationality. The collection's discovery/provenance history is complicated and partly unclear; the original paper is a primary witness but the pathway into private collections raises ethical issues.

## Named people and event — exact evidence

**Claimant:** A woman called **Fāṭima**. **Important textual discrepancy:** Bhalloo's introductory summary calls her `Fāṭima bt. Luqmān b. al-Ḥasan` (p.469); the *actual published line-6 transcription and translation* identify `Fāṭima bt. Muḥammad b. Luqmān b. al-Ḥasan` (p.471–473). Use **Fāṭima** in public copy pending editorial reconciliation; preserve both variants internally.

**Defendant:** Mīr Khwāja Muḥammad b. Ibrāhīm b. ʿAlī.

**Judge:** Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl b. Ibrāhīm b. ʿUmar.

**Dispute:** Fāṭima appeared in the qāḍī's court and claimed ownership of a **fraction of water rights**, which she said she had received from her husband Ḥusayn b. Ibrāhīm **in lieu of her mahr (marriage payment)**. She alleged the male defendant held those rights unlawfully and sought restitution/payment.

**Defense:** The defendant claimed a lawful purchase involving **70 silver dinars**. The detailed translation says he **bought from Fāṭima in the presence of her husband** and delivered payment to her husband in her presence; the *article's preceding introductory summary* instead describes buying from her husband. **Do not silently choose one account.** For public use say only that the defendant claimed a previous sale for 70 dinars and she disputed it. The witnesses' testimony recorded a sale by Fāṭima and payment to her, creating additional internal detail/interpretive issues.

**Evidence:** The defendant presented two male witnesses. The judge questioned them and required oaths affirming their testimony. The judge found in favor of the **defendant**, accepting the sale and rejecting Fāṭima's ownership claim. The record contains nine additional witness clauses attesting the proceedings, plus judge's authentication.

**What this establishes:**
- A named woman actively brought a **property rights dispute** before a qāḍī in an Islamic jurisdiction in **1212**.
- A marriage payment (mahr) and ownership of a productive resource (water access) were legally contested in a formal court.
- The court's procedure and final ruling were **recorded in a surviving contemporary document**, not only a later legend or jurist's abstract ideal.
- She **lost**. This proves capacity to initiate the case, **not equal standing across all law, favorable treatment, or effective protection in this dispute**.

**What this does *not* establish:**
- That women in every Islamic society could litigate as freely, or that gender/legal status made no difference.
- That she personally received or retained the 70 dinars; the accounts of payment **differ by summary vs translation and witnesses**.
- That the judge was fair or unfair; one record does not settle quality of adjudication or power imbalance.
- That the location is reliably the modern city of Bāmiyān, or that this woman was certainly widowed (husband's status is inferred).
- That England had no corresponding women litigants/property rights, or that premodern Islamic law is identical to modern equality.
