## 1. Source-graded core facts
**Finding A — Jesus spoke Aramaic (high-confidence, nuanced).** Historian of Aramaic Holger Gzella says Jesus likely spoke a **Galilean dialect** of Aramaic; the Galilean/Judean, Syriac and other Aramaic varieties aren't identical. Source: LMU Munich, [interview with Gzella](https://www.lmu.de/en/newsroom/news-overview/news/the-first-universal-language-70c189c7.html); Joseph A. Fitzmyer, [Did Jesus Speak Greek?](https://cojs.org/joseph-a-fitzmyer-did-jesus-speak-greek-biblical-archaeology-review-18-5-1992/). Other languages such as Hebrew and Greek were present; avoid confident monolingualism.
**Finding B — related words for God (high confidence; exact etymology nuanced).** Syriac Aramaic **ܐܠܗܐ (Alāhā)** and Aramaic script form **אלהא** denote “God / the god.” Hebrew **אֱלוֹהַּ (Elōah)** and **אֱלֹהִים (Elohim)** and Arabic **الله (Allāh)** / **إله (ilāh)** belong to a related Semitic lexical field. References: recent linguistics paper [Semitic *ʾilāh- and Hebrew אלהים](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/03090892251332286), [Cambridge excerpt Islam and Monotheism](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811089/86519/excerpt/9781108986519_excerpt.pdf), scholar Richard Kerr [Aramaisms in the Qur'an](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/Library/Kerr%2C%20R%20-%20Aramaisms%20in%20the%20Quran%20and%20their%20Significance.pdf). Do not claim these are spelled/pronounced identically, that Muslim Arabs borrowed the word straight from Jesus, or that this lexicon proves identical Christian/Muslim concepts of God. Precise original derivation of *Allāh* has competing scholarly explanations; never treat one theory as universally settled.
**Finding C — actual Biblical Jesus phrase (high confidence text, historical exact pronunciation uncertain).** Mark **15:34** preserves “Eloi, Eloi, lema sabachthani” in its Greek narrative; the Gospels preserve a few Semitic/Aramaic phrases. [NET Bible](https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php?passage=Mar+15%3A34-36); [biblical Aramaic study](https://journals.uco.es/cco/article/download/16890/15335/43281). **Do not fabricate** Jesus saying the modern Syriac Alāhā or the Arabic Allah verbatim at a specific moment. No original audio survives.
**Finding D — Arabic Christians actually say Allah (strongest direct proof).** A current Arabic Christian Bible published by [CopticChurch.net, Smith–Van Dyck Genesis 1:1](https://www.copticchurch.net/bible/arabic/SVD/Genesis/1) reads `فِي الْبَدْءِ خَلَقَ اللهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالأَرْضَ` (“In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth”). Crosscheck [Blue Letter Bible](https://www.blueletterbible.org/svd/gen/1/1-31). Christian academic/theological references [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2020/10/inshallah-biden-trump-debate-arab-christians-gods-will/) and [The Gospel Coalition](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/is-allah-god/) affirm Arabic-speaking Christians' use of `Allah`. This is the **strongest falsifiable direct audience proof**, more dependable than merely repeating the uploaded screenshot.
**Finding E — many Arab Christians say Insha'Allah (supported, not universal).** [Christianity Today: Many Arab Christians Do Too](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2020/10/inshallah-biden-trump-debate-arab-christians-gods-will/) reports actual use and that some Christian communities prefer `in sha al-rabb` (“if the Lord wills”). Academic sociolinguistic analysis [Inshallah religious invocations in Arabic topic transition](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-in-society/article/inshallah-religious-invocations-in-arabic-topic-transition/347AA792FBCE675861709EBE7CB11452) and [Arabic God-phrases across religious groups](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/12/6/412) show cultural as well as literal religious functions. Biblical [James 4:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=James%204%3A15) says “If the Lord wills”; the Qur'an [18:23–24](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=18&verse=24) likewise addresses saying one will act tomorrow contingent on God's will. **Parallel does not prove a copying path**; similar sentiments alone do not show universal Christian use of the exact Arabic phrase.
**Finding F — Jesus's Semitic words were retained in the Gospels (high-confidence text).** Mark 5:41 `Talitha koum`, Mark 7:34 `Ephphatha`, Mark 14:36 `Abba` and Mark 15:34 `Eloi` (language nuances apply). Text source [Gospel translation study](https://bibletranslation.org/en/passages/MRK/5/41/). These show a Semitic social world, not proof of later Islamic worship rites.

## 3. Analytical distinction that does the most work
- **Language:** the terms derive from related families and can be shared across religions.
- **Social practice:** vocabulary travels through worship and daily life, independent of a single group's identity.
- **Theology:** Christian Trinity/incarnation and Muslim tawhid/prophethood are significant differences, even with terms for the same Abrahamic God. “Same word” is not a test of doctrinal sameness.
- **History:** believers may hold competing interpretations and narratives of Jesus; honest source history must not settle that by semantics alone.
- **Moral pedagogy:** a skeptic who once assumed Arabic God terms “belong” only to Islam can revise that particular misconception without changing religion.
- **Civilizational memory:** scriptural and spoken languages are part of historical transmission and interreligious exchange; their persistence is a subject for bounded research, not civilizational ranking.

**Adversarial question:** If the goal is to create accurate epiphanies, what would our *strongest informed Christian critic* object to? They would correctly note that Syriac is not identical to Jesus's Galilean dialect, Trinity is not Islamic tawhid, and word overlap cannot certify revelation. Conceding all three increases credibility, not dilutes the surprise.
