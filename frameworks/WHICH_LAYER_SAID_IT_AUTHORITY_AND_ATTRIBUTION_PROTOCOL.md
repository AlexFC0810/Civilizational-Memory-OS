# Which Layer Said It? — Authority and Attribution Protocol

**Purpose:** Prevent revelation, transmitted reports, interpretation, institutions, empire, custom, and propaganda from being collapsed into one timeless speaker.

---

# Prime directive

> **Before asking whether a claim is true, ask who—or what layer—is actually speaking.**

Most historical and religious confusion begins with false attribution.

A statement may be:

- revelation;
- prophetic conduct;
- a report about prophetic conduct;
- an early communal memory;
- an exegetical judgment;
- a juristic inference;
- a school position;
- a state policy;
- an imperial practice;
- a local custom;
- a later apologetic compression;
- a hostile polemical construction;
- or a modern ideological projection.

These layers interact. They are not interchangeable.

The protocol exists to answer five questions:

1. **What layer generated the claim?**
2. **How securely can it be attributed to that layer?**
3. **What authority does that layer legitimately possess?**
4. **What power or incentive shaped its preservation and use?**
5. **What fruit did the interpretation or institution produce?**

---

# Governing maxims

> **The source is sacred. Transmission must be audited. Interpretation must be argued. Institutions must be judged by fruit.**

> **The Qur'an is not hadith. Hadith is not identical to Sunnah. Fiqh is not revelation. Empire is not Islam. Custom is not law merely because it became old.**

> **Chronological proximity can strengthen evidence. It cannot sanctify betrayal. Later distance can weaken attribution. It cannot disprove wisdom or renewal.**

> **No institution inherits the authority of revelation merely by speaking in sacred vocabulary.**

# The twelve attribution layers

## Layer 1 — Revelation

**Question:** What does the revealed text itself state?

For Islam, the Qur'an occupies the primary revelatory layer.

Required controls:

- exact text;
- recognized reading where relevant;
- immediate literary context;
- intra-Qur'anic context;
- addressee;
- circumstance or problem addressed;
- semantic range;
- stable principle versus contingent application;
- what the verse does not say.

Authority rule:

> Revelation has normative supremacy, but human claims about its meaning still require interpretation.

Failure modes:

- isolating a phrase from its passage;
- importing a later doctrine into the text;
- using translation as if it exhausted the original language;
- treating one verse as if the rest of the revelation did not exist;
- making a universal rule from a bounded conflict without proof.

## Layer 2 — Prophetic embodiment

**Question:** How did the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ live, prioritize, apply, restrain, forgive, govern, teach, and correct?

The Sunnah is the normative Prophetic way. Historical access to it is mediated through evidence.

Required controls:

- multiple independent attestations where available;
- consistency with the Qur'an;
- recurrence versus isolated event;
- public practice versus private report;
- stable conduct versus exceptional necessity;
- treatment of enemies, dependents, women, children, minorities, animals, the poor, and dissenters;
- late political use of the precedent.

Authority rule:

> The Prophetic life is normative, but not every attributed report is equally secure and not every contextual act is automatically a timeless rule.

## Layer 3 — Hadith report

**Question:** What exactly is being reported, by whom, through what chain, in which collection, and with what textual history?

Hadith are evidence about the Sunnah. They are not identical to the Sunnah itself.

Required controls:

- collection date and compiler;
- earliest recoverable appearance;
- chain structure and independence;
- transmitter evaluation;
- isnad-cum-matn comparison where available;
- wording variants;
- regional circulation;
- contradiction, corroboration, or silence;
- legal and sectarian reception;
- possible back-projection;
- modern specialist disagreement.

Authority rule:

> A hadith's practical authority depends on provenance, interpretation, relation to stronger evidence, and the methodological tradition applying it.

Do not flatten:

- mutawatir and solitary reports;
- strong and weak chains;
- widespread practice and isolated narration;
- moral wisdom and precise historical attribution;
- report authenticity and interpretation.

## Layer 4 — Sira and early historical memory

**Question:** Is this a reconstruction of the Prophetic period or early community rather than direct revelation?

Required controls:

- distance from the events;
- source dependencies;
- narrative purpose;
- court, school, sectarian, or communal context;
- comparison with documentary, epigraphic, numismatic, non-Muslim, and material evidence;
- whether a moralized story is being treated as literal chronology.

Authority rule:

> Sira can preserve indispensable memory without every detail carrying equal historical confidence.

## Layer 5 — Tafsir and theology

**Question:** Which interpreter, school, period, and method produced this reading?

Required controls:

- commentator and date;
- linguistic method;
- theological commitments;
- cited reports;
- competing interpretations;
- relationship to political authority;
- later reception;
- whether consensus is claimed accurately.

Authority rule:

> Tafsir is disciplined human interpretation, not revelation repeating itself.

## Layer 6 — Fiqh and legal method

**Question:** Is this a juristic conclusion, and what legal reasoning produced it?

Required controls:

- school and jurist;
- hierarchy of sources;
- evidentiary threshold;
- analogy, custom, necessity, public welfare, blocking means, or purposes invoked;
- majority, minority, and dissenting positions;
- theory versus actual court practice;
- historical conditions addressed;
- modern applicability.

Authority rule:

> Fiqh is accumulated legal intelligence with a rebuttable presumption of serious labor—not divine infallibility.

Do not collapse:

- one jurist into a school;
- one school into Islam;
- ideal doctrine into uniform enforcement;
- medieval governance assumptions into timeless necessity;
- legal permissibility into moral excellence.

## Layer 7 — Madhhab and scholarly institution

**Question:** Did a school preserve a method, or did its institutional survival harden one answer into unquestionable identity?

Required controls:

- formation history;
- internal diversity;
- teaching and credentialing systems;
- state patronage or suppression;
- geographic variation;
- capacity for correction;
- what the school protected that individual improvisation might lose;
- where institutional self-preservation distorted purpose.

Authority rule:

> A school can preserve distributed intelligence. It must still remain answerable to revelation, evidence, purpose, and fruit.

## Layer 8 — State law and ruler policy

**Question:** Was this an Islamic norm, a ruler's selection among norms, an emergency policy, fiscal strategy, or naked power using religious language?

Required controls:

- issuing authority;
- jurisdiction and enforcement;
- political and fiscal incentives;
- juristic support and opposition;
- duration and geographic scope;
- gap between decree and practice;
- who benefited;
- who could challenge it;
- whether later memory retroactively sanctified it.

Authority rule:

> State action is historical evidence about Muslim governance. It is not self-authenticating evidence of Islam.

## Layer 9 — Empire and civilizational practice

**Question:** Is the claim about what Muslim empires or societies did rather than what Islam required?

Required controls:

- dynasty, region, century, and institution;
- administrative inheritance from Byzantium, Persia, India, Africa, Central Asia, or elsewhere;
- Muslim and non-Muslim actors;
- comparative contemporary baseline;
- variation across class, gender, religion, and locality;
- durable fruit and recurring betrayal;
- direct, indirect, or merely compatible relationship to revelation.

Authority rule:

> Civilizational fruit can reveal what a moral architecture enabled. It cannot retroactively turn every historical practice into revelation.

## Layer 10 — Local culture and inherited custom

**Question:** Is this Arab, Persian, Turkish, South Asian, African, European, tribal, patriarchal, class-based, or national custom wearing an Islamic label?

Required controls:

- pre-Islamic continuity;
- regional comparison;
- legal recognition or opposition;
- elite versus popular practice;
- migration and diaspora effects;
- whether people mistake familiarity for sanctity.

Authority rule:

> Custom may be legitimate, tolerated, neutral, harmful, or prohibited. Age alone does not confer revelation-level authority.

## Layer 11 — Propaganda and apologetic compression

**Question:** Is the claim designed to mobilize identity rather than preserve complexity?

Forms include:

- imperial legitimation;
- sectarian myth;
- colonial historiography;
- nationalist memory;
- missionary polemic;
- anti-Muslim media;
- defensive Muslim apologetics;
- influencer compression;
- algorithmic outrage.

Required controls:

- intended audience;
- emotional trigger;
- omitted context;
- source laundering;
- repetition network;
- political benefit;
- asymmetric standards;
- whether a true fact is being used to imply a false totality.

Authority rule:

> A repeated claim is evidence of a memory system, not proof of the event described.

## Layer 12 — Modern reconstruction and renewal

**Question:** Is a modern scholar or movement recovering the root, projecting modern values backward, or creating a new synthesis?

Required controls:

- explicit method;
- source base;
- relationship to inherited scholarship;
- modern political and moral incentives;
- anachronism risk;
- continuity and rupture;
- institutional feasibility;
- correction mechanisms;
- fruit among people with least leverage.

Authority rule:

> Renewal is not validated by novelty or by claims of purity. It must demonstrate fidelity, explanatory power, institutional wisdom, and corrigibility.

# The attribution decision tree

For every disputed claim, run this order.

## Step 1 — Freeze the wording

Write the exact claim without improving or weakening it.

Example structure:

> “Islam commands X in all times and places.”

Then identify hidden subclaims:

- textual;
- historical;
- legal;
- causal;
- comparative;
- moral;
- universal.

## Step 2 — Name the claimed speaker

Ask:

- Does the speaker mean the Qur'an?
- the Prophet ﷺ?
- a hadith collection?
- classical jurists?
- one jurist?
- a ruler?
- a dynasty?
- Muslims in one region?
- modern Islamists?
- popular culture?

If the speaker cannot identify the layer, the claim is not ready for adjudication.

## Step 3 — Locate the earliest evidence

Record:

- earliest text;
- earliest manuscript or documentary witness where relevant;
- earliest known legal formulation;
- earliest institutional implementation;
- earliest hostile or outsider witness;
- major gaps or silences.

## Step 4 — Audit transmission

Ask:

- How many independent routes exist?
- Are they genuinely independent?
- Did wording stabilize early or late?
- Does the report appear where we would expect it?
- What incentives favored preservation, alteration, or invention?

## Step 5 — Audit interpretation

Ask:

- What semantic choices are contested?
- Which school or method produced the conclusion?
- What stronger or parallel evidence constrains it?
- Is a contextual judgment being universalized?

## Step 6 — Audit power

Ask:

- Who gained legitimacy, wealth, status, immunity, or control?
- Who paid the cost?
- Could vulnerable people challenge the interpretation?
- Did the state select one view because it was true, administratively convenient, or politically useful?

## Step 7 — Audit implementation

Separate:

- theory;
- law on the books;
- court practice;
- administrative enforcement;
- popular custom;
- exceptional abuse;
- systematic pattern.

## Step 8 — Run the fruit test

Ask what the interpretation repeatedly produced in:

- ruler accountability;
- protection of children;
- women’s standing;
- treatment of minorities;
- poverty and debt;
- war and defeated populations;
- slavery and dependency;
- knowledge institutions;
- animal welfare;
- correction and dissent.

Fruit does not replace source evidence. It reveals whether an interpretation preserved or inverted the root.

## Step 9 — Classify the outcome

Use the canonical matrix:

- faithful preservation;
- faithful development;
- contextual translation;
- partial implementation;
- cultural accretion;
- institutional hardening;
- political capture;
- sacred inversion;
- prophetic renewal.

## Step 10 — Issue a bounded verdict

Every verdict must state:

1. the layer actually speaking;
2. confidence in attribution;
3. authority of the layer;
4. scope by time and place;
5. strongest competing interpretation;
6. what would change the verdict;
7. whether public compression is permitted.

# Authority is not a single score

Use four separate ratings.

## A. Attribution confidence

How likely is it that the text, act, or policy genuinely belongs to the claimed source?

- A — exceptionally strong
- B — strong
- C — plausible but contested
- D — weak
- E — unsupported or misattributed

## B. Normative authority

How much authority does the layer possess within the tradition?

- 5 — revelation
- 4 — securely established Prophetic norm
- 3 — strong inherited interpretation or consensus claim requiring specification
- 2 — juristic, institutional, or scholarly judgment
- 1 — state policy, custom, or historical practice
- 0 — propaganda, fabrication, or external attribution

## C. Scope confidence

How confidently can the claim be generalized?

- universal principle
- recurring transregional pattern
- bounded school position
- regional or dynastic practice
- local case
- exceptional event

## D. Fruit integrity

Did implementation preserve the moral purpose?

- regenerative
- substantially faithful
- mixed
- extractive
- inverted
- unknown

Do not combine these into one prestige number. A report can have strong attribution but weak universal scope. A later institution can have low revelatory authority but excellent functional fidelity.

# Worked pattern 1 — A warfare verse

Bad attribution:

> “Islam tells Muslims to kill unbelievers.”

Protocol:

1. Identify the verse and passage.
2. Identify addressee, conflict, treaty status, persecution, expulsion, and surrounding restraints.
3. Distinguish Qur'anic authorization from later juristic expansion.
4. Distinguish juristic doctrine from imperial practice.
5. Test whether the claim erases peace, covenant, non-transgression, or justice toward non-hostile peoples.
6. Identify whether later states converted a contingent conflict into permanent ideology.
7. Issue a verse-specific, doctrine-specific, and history-specific verdict.

The protocol does not pre-decide the answer. It prevents one layer from impersonating all others.

# Worked pattern 2 — Aisha’s age

Bad attribution:

> “The Qur'an says Aisha was nine.”

Immediate correction:

- the Qur'an does not state her age;
- the claim arises from hadith reports and later historical chronology;
- report provenance, textual development, biographical synchronisms, and specialist disagreement must be audited separately;
- legal and moral conclusions are additional layers, not contained automatically in the report.

Required separations:

1. What do the reports say?
2. How early and independent are they?
3. What alternative chronological evidence exists?
4. How did premodern scholars receive them?
5. How did historical societies define childhood, puberty, capacity, guardianship, and marriage?
6. What, if anything, follows normatively today?

# Worked pattern 3 — Women’s dress

Bad attribution:

> “Islam requires every woman to cover everything except an eye slit.”

Protocol:

- identify Qur'anic language and addressee;
- distinguish modesty commands to men and women;
- distinguish Qur'an, hadith, tafsir, fiqh, regional dress, state enforcement, and modern ideological symbolism;
- map school differences;
- separate minimum legal arguments from recommended piety and local custom;
- evaluate coercion, dignity, public order, and moral fruit.

# Worked pattern 4 — A ruler’s cruelty

Bad attribution:

> “A Muslim ruler did X; therefore Islam commands X.”

Protocol:

- identify ruler, date, jurisdiction, and act;
- identify contemporary juristic support and opposition;
- locate political and fiscal incentives;
- compare the act with revelation and Prophetic restraint;
- determine whether the act was exceptional, normalized, resisted, or later sanctified;
- classify as implementation, capture, or inversion.

# Red-team questions

Before publishing any major verdict, ask:

1. Are we attributing a later report directly to the Qur'an?
2. Are we treating one hadith as the whole Sunnah?
3. Are we treating one school as all Islam?
4. Are we treating a ruler’s policy as sacred law?
5. Are we treating imperial expansion as identical to Qur'anic purpose?
6. Are we treating local patriarchy as timeless theology?
7. Are we treating historical Muslim failure as proof that no higher standard existed?
8. Are we treating the higher standard as proof that Muslims consistently obeyed it?
9. Are we accepting an early date as proof of moral fidelity?
10. Are we dismissing later scholarship merely because it is later?
11. Are we using a modern moral category without defining its historical analogue?
12. Are we comparing Islamic ideals with another civilization’s failures—or vice versa?
13. Have we identified the strongest informed critic?
14. Can the vulnerable testify against our synthesis?
15. What evidence would force us to revise the verdict?

# Integration with the Civilizational Fruit Evidence Matrix

This protocol answers **who spoke and with what authority**.

The Fruit Matrix answers **what the claim produced, how distinctive it was, and whether it is ready for public deployment**.

Required sequence:

> **Attribution → provenance → interpretation → power → implementation → fruit → comparison → compression**

A claim may not enter Level 3 public-ready status until its layer is named and its authority is bounded.

A claim may not enter Level 4 streamer compression if the compression causes a lower layer to impersonate revelation.

# Canonical output format

Every finished adjudication should be able to produce this paragraph:

> **This claim does not come directly from [revelation / the Prophet / the named source]. It arises at the layer of [report / interpretation / school / state / custom / propaganda]. Its attribution is [confidence], its legitimate authority is [bounded description], and its historical scope is [time and place]. The strongest evidence supports [verdict], while [competing view] remains material because [reason]. The decisive fruit and power question is [question].**

Then compress only after the full record exists.

# Closing doctrine

> **Which layer said it?** is not a rhetorical escape hatch.

It cannot be used to dismiss inconvenient history, evade authentic Prophetic evidence, or declare every harmful Muslim practice “not real Islam” without adjudication.

Its purpose is stricter:

> **Never grant revelation responsibility for a later layer without proof. Never protect a later layer from judgment by allowing it to impersonate revelation. Never discard accumulated wisdom merely because it is human.**

The result is neither blind traditionalism nor amnesiac revisionism.

It is a civilization capable of remembering accurately enough to correct itself.
