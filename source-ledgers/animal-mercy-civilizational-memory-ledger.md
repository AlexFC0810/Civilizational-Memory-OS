# 2. Essential academic scholarship

**Use:** Primary scholarly anchor for Qur'anic animal ontology, hierarchy, community, worship, human status, and eschatology.

**Priority:** Read fully before publishing major claims about the Qur'an's theory of animals.

## Richard C. Foltz — *Animals in Islamic Tradition and Muslim Cultures*

**Publisher:** Oneworld Publications, 2006  
**Publisher page:** https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Animals-in-Islamic-Tradition-and-Muslim-Cultures/Richard-Foltz/9781851683987

**Use:** Broad map across Qur'an, hadith, jurisprudence, philosophy, literature, mysticism, and contemporary Muslim practice.

**Boundary:** Use as orientation; return to specialist and primary sources for proof claims.

## Al-Hafiz Basheer Ahmad Masri — *Animal Welfare in Islam*

**Publisher:** Islamic Foundation; multiple editions  
**Bibliographic interface:** https://books.google.com/books/about/Animal_Welfare_in_Islam.html?id=97NAAQAAIAAJ

**Use:** Foundational modern Muslim ethical collection.

**Boundary:** Verify each quotation and report independently before publication.

## Alan Mikhail — *The Animal in Ottoman Egypt*

**Publisher:** Oxford University Press, 2013/2014  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199315277.001.0001  
**Publisher page:** https://academic.oup.com/book/3898

**Use:** Essential environmental history demonstrating that animals were constitutive of labor, urban life, political economy, ecology, and state transformation in Ottoman Egypt.

**Safe claim:**

> Human-animal relations were central rather than peripheral to major Ottoman Egyptian social, economic, ecological, and political transformations.

**Boundary:** This work does not automatically verify popular claims about animal hospitals, retired-animal pensions, or particular waqfs.

# 3. Current animal-consciousness evidence

## New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness

**Declaration:** https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration  
**Background and research map:** https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/background  
**Date:** April 19, 2024

**High-confidence summary:**

- strong scientific support for conscious experience in mammals and birds;
- at least a realistic possibility in all vertebrates and many invertebrates;
- uncertainty should not justify ignoring welfare risks.

**Boundary:** This is a scientific declaration signed by experts in their individual capacities, not proof that all species possess identical forms of experience.

## Marino and Colvin — “Thinking Pigs”

**Journal:** *International Journal of Comparative Psychology*, 28, 2015  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.46867/ijcp.2015.28.00.04  
**Open repository:** https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8sx4s79c

**Use:** Strong direct source on pig cognition, emotion, personality, social capacities, and comparative psychology.

**Safe claim:**

> Pigs are cognitively and emotionally complex mammals, not inert biological automata.

# 4. Childhood formation, imitation, and intervention

## Bandura, Ross, and Ross — modeled aggression

**Article:** “Transmission of Aggression Through Imitation of Aggressive Models”  
**Journal:** *Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology*, 63(3), 575–582, 1961  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1037/h0045925  
**PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13864605/

**Use:** Foundational evidence that children can reproduce modeled aggressive behavior.

**Boundary:** Does not prove that every child witnessing animal slaughter becomes cruel or that one exposure causes lasting desensitization.

## Frank R. Ascione — childhood animal cruelty review

**Article:** “Children Who Are Cruel to Animals: A Review of Research and Implications for Developmental Psychopathology”  
**Journal:** *Anthrozoös*, 6(4), 226–247, 1993  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2752/089279393787002105  
**Government abstract:** https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/children-who-are-cruel-animals-review-research-and-implications

**Use:** Animal cruelty as a potential safeguarding and developmental warning signal linked in some contexts with wider violence.

**Boundary:** Association is not destiny; never label a child as inevitably violent.

## Hawkins et al. — primary-school animal-welfare education

**Article:** “Assessing Effectiveness of a Nonhuman Animal Welfare Education Program for Primary School Children”  
**PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28368662/

**Use:** Direct evidence that animal-welfare education can improve relevant knowledge and some beliefs about animal minds.

## Ngai et al. — Hong Kong humane-education pilot

**Journal:** *PLOS ONE*, 16(3), e0249033, 2021  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0249033  
**Open article:** https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0249033

**Use:** Controlled pilot suggesting improvement in empathy and social-emotional competence, with implementation caveats.

## “Learning to Care” randomized trial

**Journal:** *International Journal of Educational Research Open*, 5, 100292, 2023  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijedro.2023.100292

**Use:** Randomized evidence that sustained animal- and nature-centered education can improve affective and cognitive empathy among young children.

**Boundary:** A year-long program is not proof that a single short lesson will yield equivalent effects.

## Humane education and violence prevention review

**Journal:** *Children and Youth Services Review*, 32(3), 365–370, 2010  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.childyouth.2009.10.006

**Use:** Connects humane education with empathy formation, early intervention, and violence prevention.

# 6. Historical claims under quarantine

The following claims must not become public proof until archival or specialist evidence is attached:

- Ottoman waqfs specifically paying for retired horses, donkeys, or camels;
- formal stork hospitals as a widespread institutional category;
- animal pensions after working usefulness;
- named citywide endowments feeding cats or dogs;
- “the world's first animal hospital” claims;
- precise claims that Islamic animal protection preceded Europe by a stated number of centuries;
- universal claims about Muslim treatment of animals.

## Required proof standard

For each claim, secure at least one of:

1. primary waqf deed, court register, state record, inscription, chronicle, or comparable contemporaneous document;
2. university-press or peer-reviewed archival scholarship citing identifiable records;
3. two independent specialist sources with traceable references.

Travel accounts and local tradition may corroborate. They should not carry a flagship claim alone.

# 7. Comparative research agenda

## Islamic source architecture

- How did jurists define duties to owned, working, hunted, sacrificed, dangerous, and stray animals?
- Which duties were enforceable in courts, and which were spiritual or ethical counsel?
- How did Sunni, Shia, Ibadi, philosophical, Sufi, and regional traditions differ?

## Institutional fruit

- Which waqf deeds explicitly name animals or animal-related public goods?
- What veterinary, market, slaughter, transport, and municipal regulations existed?
- What happened to animals after economic usefulness ended?
- Were protections episodic, regional, elite, popular, or systemically recurring?

## Comparative moral history

- What parallel protections existed in Byzantine, Latin Christian, Jewish, South Asian, East Asian, African, and Indigenous systems?
- When comparing dates, are we comparing scripture, ethical counsel, enforceable law, institutions, or observed practice?
- Which systems linked animal mercy to divine judgment, civic virtue, sentience, utility, rights, or ecological harmony?

## Modern renewal

- Which contemporary Muslim institutions embody the source architecture rather than merely cite it?
- Can mosque and school formation produce measurable protective competence?
- How should halal systems audit handling, transport, housing, stunning, slaughter, and worker incentives?
- What would a modern animal-care waqf look like?

# 8. Civilizational claim card

## Claim

> Islamic revelation contains an unusually compressed animal-mercy architecture in which nonhuman creatures possess moral significance, mercy toward them can carry immense spiritual reward, cruelty can carry grave accountability, and permitted use remains governed by ihsan.

## Status

**Strongly supported normative claim.**

## What it does not prove

- flawless historical Muslim behavior;
- superiority across every animal-welfare metric;
- existence of every popular Ottoman mercy story;
- automatic translation of doctrine into institutions.

## Historical question

> Under what conditions did this source architecture become repeated practice, enforceable norm, funded institution, or durable civilizational fruit?

## Renewal question

> How can the source architecture be rebuilt into measurable formation and protection now?

# 9. Canonical synthesis

> **The strongest historical case is neither that Muslims uniquely invented concern for animals nor that Islamic teaching remained merely sentimental. The source architecture is unusually serious: animals are placed inside a God-conscious moral universe; hidden cruelty can carry judgment; small mercy can carry salvation; and utility does not cancel ihsan. Modern scholarship and science allow us to study the blueprint, animal experience, historical implementation, and educational renewal separately. Civilizational memory must preserve all four—and refuse to promote folklore into proof.**
