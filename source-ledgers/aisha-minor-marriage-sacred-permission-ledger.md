## Evidence Rules

1. Distinguish the Qur'an from hadith, sira, fiqh, court records, and modern reconstruction.
2. Distinguish traditional authenticity from recoverable historical certainty.
3. Distinguish legal validity from demographic prevalence.
4. Distinguish origin from later legitimation.
5. Mark every causal claim by confidence level.
6. Do not claim that 'A'isha was definitely eighteen.
7. Do not claim that no classical scholar understood Qur'an 65:4 as including minors.
8. Do not infer universal Muslim practice from jurisprudential permission.
9. Do not allow historical uncertainty to become contemporary permission over children.

## Primary Qur'anic Texts

### Qur'an 4:6

Research questions:

- How do major tafsirs define *balaghu al-nikah*?
- How do they distinguish biological puberty, marriageability, and *rushd*?
- Does any school explicitly apply *rushd* to marital consent, or only property?
- How can the verse's anthropology inform policy without falsely presenting it as a numerical statute?

### Qur'an 4:19

Research questions:

- How are will, compulsion, inheritance of women, and *ma'ruf* treated?
- Which jurists used this verse in consent doctrine?

### Qur'an 4:21

Research questions:

- What does *mithaqan ghaliza* imply in classical and modern exegesis?
- What minimum capacity is conceptually required to enter a solemn covenant?

### Qur'an 30:21

Research questions:

- How do tranquility, affection, and mercy operate as legal purposes?
- How have modern family-law reformers used these purposes?

### Qur'an 65:4

Research requirements:

- Map major Sunni and Shi'i tafsirs chronologically.
- Record explicit interpretation of *lam yahidna*.
- Distinguish absence of menstruation due to youth, illness, or other condition.
- Distinguish regulation of divorce from recommendation of marriage.
- Identify modern reinterpretations and evaluate whether they fit the Arabic and reception history.

## Social-History Guardrail

Every claim about practice must identify:

- place;
- century;
- class;
- source type;
- sample limitations;
- whether the record shows contract age, cohabitation age, or both;
- whether ages were recorded contemporaneously or reconstructed.

Do not use fiqh manuals as population data.

Do not use one court archive as representative of the entire Muslim world.

## Capture Card

### Claim

“The Prophet married 'A'isha at nine, therefore Islam permanently permits marriage at nine.”

### Source layer

- No Qur'anic age claim.
- Later hadith report with canonical Sunni status.

### Historical layer

- Exact age unresolved.
- Serious modern challenge to report's emergence and dating.

### Legal layer

- Minor-marriage doctrine existed through guardian-centered jurisprudence.
- Report later used as supporting Prophetic precedent.

### Institutional layer

- Courts and family law preserved pathways variably across time and place.

### Memory layer

- Permission increasingly remembered as inseparable from fidelity to Muhammad.

### Beneficiary layer

- guardians;
- adult husbands;
- kinship systems;
- institutions protecting inherited authority.

### Vulnerability layer

- girls bear bodily, psychological, educational, reproductive, and economic risk.

### Fruit test

- Does the rule produce adult covenantal agency, mercy, education, and protection?
- Or does it transfer uncertainty and risk onto the least powerful person?

### Corrective principle

> **Historical uncertainty must never be converted into coercive certainty over vulnerable people.**

## Public-Claim Safety Rules

Never publish:

- “'A'isha was definitely eighteen.”
- “Bukhari invented the report.”
- “No early Muslim accepted child marriage.”
- “Qur'an 65:4 has nothing to do with minors.”
- “All Muslim societies routinely married nine-year-olds.”
- “The hadith report alone caused child marriage.”

Preferred formulations:

- “The traditional report says six and nine, but modern historical criticism gives serious reasons to doubt that it preserves secure chronology.”
- “The evidence does not allow confident recovery of her exact age.”
- “Minor marriage predated Islam; Muslim jurists later regulated and sometimes sacralized it.”
- “Legal permission varied from actual social prevalence.”
- “Qur'an 65:4 regulates a waiting-period category; it does not command child marriage or establish nine as a universal age.”
