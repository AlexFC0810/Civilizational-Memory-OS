# Source Card 021: The Muslim Keyholders of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre

Date: 2026-08-02

## Status

**Deployment status:** provenance-audited draft through the mechanical gate; graded B, with the origin attributions graded separately and lower inside the body. Hardened against three refutation lenses (primary-source, hostile-historian, category-collapse) on 2026-08-02.

**Best use:** bridge-native material for the islamlovesjesus.org lane — one of the few artifacts in this archive that runs **offensively** (a positive story told on its own terms) rather than defensively (a rebuttal). Precisely because it travels well, it is graded harder than a rebuttal card, not softer: a clip that asserts "since Umar in 637" as settled fact hands an opponent a free correction, and the correction is easy to find because *the custodian families' own published histories contradict each other.*

**House style — the two firewalls this card exists to enforce:**

1. **Gate the confident half; declare the origin contested.** The arrangement is real, observed, and documented by outside parties for at least a century. *When it began and who began it is genuinely disputed* — and not as a tidy two-way dispute. There are at least four mutually inconsistent versions in the sources, two of them published by the two custodian families about each other.
2. **Do not sentimentalize the mechanism.** The custody persists substantially because the six Christian communions sharing the church operate under a Status Quo requiring unanimous consent, which makes a custodian belonging to none of the six the practical solution. That is a *more* interesting and more defensible story than "Muslims kindly guarded a church," and it is the honest one. Say both: the arrangement is real and remarkable, **and** its durability owes much to inter-denominational deadlock, not only to Muslim goodwill.

Pairs with Card 008 (`008_tolerance_comparative_history.md`) and Card 016 (`016_medina_covenant.md`) — but note the direction of the pairing: this card **limits** what those may claim, because the same building carries a Muslim ruler's demolition order (1009) and the same arrangement was maintained by non-Muslim sovereigns for the last century.

## Claim

Each morning at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem — the church built over the sites Christians identify as the crucifixion and burial of Jesus — a member of the Muslim **Joudeh al-Goudia** family brings the key to the building's single main door, and a member of the Muslim **Nuseibeh** family uses it to unlock the door and, in the evening, to lock it again. Both are Palestinian Muslim households of Jerusalem; the office is hereditary; the practice is performed daily and has been recorded by parties outside both families for at least a century. The confident half of this story and the disputed half are usually shipped together as one, and they must be separated.

**Well attested — this card's graded load.** The practice as it now operates, and its institutional setting. Six Christian communions — Greek Orthodox, Roman Catholic (the Franciscan Custody of the Holy Land), Armenian Apostolic, Coptic, Syriac Orthodox, and Ethiopian Orthodox — hold overlapping and competing rights inside the building under the **Status Quo**, traced to an Ottoman firman of 1757 and reaffirmed by firmans of 1852 and 1853 which fixed that no change may be made without the consent of all six communities. L. G. A. Cust's British Mandate survey of the Status Quo (published 1929) marks on its plan of the church that "Moslem custodians have a divan at the entrance" — an official record of the practice, written by a non-Muslim administrator with no stake in either family's account. The arrangement has continued across every sovereignty change since: Ottoman, British Mandate, Jordanian, and Israeli rule since 1967.

**Genuinely disputed — this card refuses to settle it.** When the arrangement began, and by whose act. Three attributions circulate; the sources yield at least four mutually inconsistent versions.

- **Caliph Umar, 637/638.** The Nuseibeh family's own tradition traces the office to Umar entrusting the church to the Prophet's companion 'Ubadah ibn al-Samit at the Muslim capture of Jerusalem. Against it: the encyclopaedic summary records that the family "themselves admit the documents held by various Christian denominations only mention their role since the 12th century in the time of Saladin, which is the more generally accepted date." The surviving text of Umar's Assurance protects churches but says nothing about doors, keys, or any custodian family — and its authenticity is itself disputed among historians, some of whom argue the Christian recensions were forged by Christian scribes to secure possession of sites.
- **Saladin, 1187 or 1192.** The more generally accepted attribution, and the one the Joudeh family cites, via the Jerusalem historian Aref al-Aref. Against it: the Nuseibeh family's own published history dates their ancestor's investiture to 1192, not 1187; and the text of the 1192 Saladin–Richard peace, which guarantees Christians "free passage and access to the Holy Sepulcher of the Lord without the exaction of any tribute," says nothing whatever about the church's doors, keys, or the appointment of any custodian.
- **Ottoman formalization.** The Nuseibeh family's own published history states flatly that "The Judeh family have no relationships with the Church of the Holy Sepulchre prior to 1611 ad. when its founder obtained a fireman, a certificate from Istanbul." The Joudeh family's own site dates itself to 1187. **The two partner families' own published accounts contradict each other by more than four centuries about when one of them began.** No firman naming either family has been located, published, or catalogued by anyone outside the families.

**The mechanism, stated honestly.** The arrangement's durability owes at least as much to Christian inter-communal rivalry as to Muslim goodwill. The Status Quo exists *because the communions fought*: the 1757 decree followed a Holy Week in which Orthodox Christians "took over some of the Franciscan-controlled church"; before that settlement, control of the sites "oscillated between the Catholic (Latin) and the Orthodox (Greek) churches, depending upon which could obtain a favorable firman (decree) from the Ottoman Sublime Porte at a particular time, often through outright bribery"; the Holy Places dispute fed into the Crimean War; a wooden ladder leaning against a window since at least the 1720s has never been moved because moving it would require all six to agree; and clergy of the six communions have come to blows inside the building into the 21st century. Under a rule that freezes every arrangement until all six consent, a custodian belonging to none of the six is the practical solution — and no single communion can be advantaged at the door. Yisca Harani, a scholar of Christianity in the Holy Land, told Reuters the church "is definitely a model of co-existence." It is. The model's engine is deadlock as much as trust.

**And the record is not one of unbroken protection.** On 18 October 1009 the Fatimid caliph al-Hakim ordered this same church destroyed; reconstruction was undertaken only in 1042, by a Byzantine emperor, with the permission of al-Hakim's successor. The Nuseibeh family's own history concedes "an interval of 100 years in which the relationship was disrupted by the crusader invasion." A version of this card that omitted either fact would be advocacy wearing an archive's clothes.

**What this does not establish.** It does not establish how Muslim rulers treated Christians in general — it is one arrangement, at one building, in one city, and the same building carries a Muslim ruler's demolition order. It does not establish that Islamic law or scripture prescribes such custodianship; no fiqh ruling and no scriptural text is in evidence here, which is why this card's layer is `culture` and not `institution` or `fiqh`. It does not establish that the Christian communions are uniquely quarrelsome; contested sacred space produces this behaviour wherever several communities hold deep claims to one site. And it is not evidence for the superiority of any religion: the arrangement has been maintained in turn by Muslim, Christian, and Jewish sovereigns — which is the strongest reason it cannot be read as the expression of any one of them.

## Evidence Grade

**B** — for the claim as scoped in the frontmatter: the present-day operation of the arrangement, its six-communion Status Quo setting, its continuation across four sovereignties, and the fact that its origin is disputed. Eighteen successful live fetches across twelve publishers, with three genuinely independent witness lines (below) and every recorded variance carried rather than smoothed.

**No origin date is graded B.** Graded separately, and lower:

- **Umar / 637–638: D.** Family tradition only. Contradicted by the custodian family's own admission that the Christian-held documents begin with Saladin; the surviving text of Umar's Assurance says nothing about doors or keys; the Assurance's own authenticity is contested; and no chronicle passage attesting a 7th-century doorkeeping office was located.
- **Saladin / 1187 or 1192: C.** Tertiary consensus with no primary document reached. The two custodian families disagree (1187 vs 1192) about the same investiture; the 1192 treaty text contains nothing about doors, keys, or custodians; the named medieval witness (Mujir al-Din) could not be confirmed in the standard accessible translation of his chronicle; the widely republished day-level dates are uncited and one of them is chronologically impossible.
- **Ottoman formalization: C.** The general Status Quo firman chain (1757, 1852, 1853, Treaty of Paris 1856, Treaty of Berlin 1878) is well corroborated. But no firman naming either family has been located, published, or catalogued outside family custody, and the two families' own accounts of when the Ottoman-era arrangement began differ by more than four centuries.

## Honest Caveats

1. **Never assert a specific origin date as settled.** This is the single most likely failure mode for this material. Three attributions circulate and at least four inconsistent versions exist in the sources, two of them published by the two custodian families about each other. The correct deploy form is "for centuries" plus "accounts differ on when it began," or an explicit enumeration of the competing dates. 2. **The origin question is identity-charged for the claimant — treat family self-report as testimony, not archive.** In 1966 Anwar Zaki Nuseibeh declined a medal from the Greek Orthodox Archbishop because the accompanying certificate dated the family's custody to Saladin rather than to Umar. 3. **Do not describe the arrangement as a gift of trust.** The living custodian's own word is "appointed."
