# Source Card 027: Righteousness Over Lineage — Qur'an 49:13 and the Hierarchy That Followed

Date: 2026-08-18

## Status

**Deployment status:** reconnaissance-closure draft through the mechanical gate, graded **B**. The Qur'anic layer and the Ibn Khaldun passages are A-strength and were fetched verbatim; every breaker at the institutional and legal layer is encyclopedic and therefore caps the whole. Closure stays `reconnaissance`. This card carries the archive's Load-Bearing Index entry **LB-04** and promotes it from Tier 2 to Tier 1.

**Why this card exists, and why its valence is `failure`.** LB-04 has sat at Tier 2 worded as a norm claim with an adherence caveat attached: *"Qur'an 49:13 recognizes peoples and tribes but makes righteousness, not lineage, the measure of nobility — even though Muslim societies have had ethnic hierarchies and racism in practice."* That wording puts the norm in the thesis and the failure in a subordinate clause. Carding it tested that framing against the evidence, and the evidence does not support it. The norm is not in dispute — the verse says what it says, the exegetical tradition read it the way the index says, and no serious accuser denies it. What this card actually establishes, and what was not already granted, is the size and depth of the gap: **lineage rank was not merely tolerated in Muslim societies, it was written into the tradition's own law and into the pages of its greatest historian.** The operative conclusion is therefore the violation, not the standard. Per `research-ledger/CANONICAL_CLAIM_RECORD.md` — *"assign it to what the card argues, never to what the archive needs"* — the honest label is **`failure`**, and it would remain `failure` if the corpus already leaned that way.

**Why this is not the same shape as CMOS-0001, which is `corrective`.** The objection deserves a direct answer, because an inconsistent standard would corrupt the metric as surely as a flattering one. CMOS-0001 (women's property and inheritance) carries a norm plus an adherence gap and is labelled `corrective`; the gap there sits at the `outcome` and `culture` layers while the substantive right survived in fiqh. Here the gap penetrates the `fiqh` layer itself: classical Sunni political theory made descent from Quraysh a qualification for the highest office in the community, and two of the four Sunni schools counted lineage among the compatibility criteria of marriage law. That is not a society falling short of its jurists. That is the jurists encoding the very axis the verse denies. The distinction is derived from the evidence, not chosen to move a number.

**Layer derivation.** The index's pre-carding estimate was `quran`. The evidence does not confirm it. What is graded here is not a proposition about the Qur'anic text alone; it is a proposition about the *relation* between a source-layer norm and the fiqh, institutional, cultural and outcome layers that were supposed to carry it — a relation that runs in both directions, since the verse also supplied the vocabulary its opponents used to protest. That is what `synthesis` is for, and it matches CMOS-0011, which has the same Layer-1-text-to-Layer-3-function shape. The `quran` estimate was not wrong about where the norm lives; it was wrong about what the card concludes.

**Best use:** as the archive's answer to two opposite accusations at once, without softening either. To the claim that Islam is Arab supremacy in religious dress, the answer is the verse, its exegesis, and the fact that the protest movement against Arab privilege took its name from the verse's own word for "peoples." To the claim that Muslim societies were free of racial hierarchy, the answer is Yazid II restoring the tax on non-Arab Muslims, the Qurashi qualification, the kafa'ah lineage criterion, and the Muqaddimah. **House style:** the failure first and unhedged, in the first sentence. The standard is in the card at full strength — and it is stated as what the evidence makes it, an indictment the civilization wrote and then failed, not a defence that cancels the failure.

## Claim

Islamic civilization possessed an explicit, unambiguous repudiation of lineage-based rank at its textual foundation, and built ethnic hierarchy anyway — in its fiscal administration, in its jurisprudence, in its slave trade, and in the work of its most celebrated historian. That is this card's thesis, and nothing in the card makes it smaller.

**The standard, stated first, because everything after is measured against it.** Qur'an 49:13 reads, in M.A.S. Abdel Haleem's translation fetched from the quran.com API this session: "People, We created you all from a single man and a single woman, and made you into races and tribes so that you should recognize one another. In God's eyes, the most honoured of you are the ones most mindful of Him: God is all knowing, all aware." Saheeh International, fetched independently, renders the operative clause "the most noble of you in the sight of Allāh is the most righteous of you." The Arabic is unambiguous on the point that matters: *inna akramakum 'inda-llāhi atqākum* — the criterion is *taqwā*, and the plural nouns it displaces are *shu'ūban wa-qabā'ila*, peoples and tribes. Two adjacent verses generalize it: 30:22 makes "the diversity of your languages and colours" a sign of God rather than a ranking, and 49:11 forbids one group jeering at another and forbids offensive nicknames. The classical exegetical tradition read the verse the way the plain sense suggests: Tafsir Ibn Kathir, fetched from the same API, glosses the clause as meaning "you earn honor with Allah the Exalted on account of Taqwa, not family lineage," and adduces a hadith it attributes to al-Bukhari in which the Prophet, asked who is most honourable, answers "the one who has the most Taqwa" — and, pressed a third time about *Arab* lineage specifically, redirects to conduct and understanding.

**The standard was violated as governing policy within the first Islamic century.** Under the Umayyads, according to the fetched encyclopedic record, "the Arab Muslims held themselves in higher esteem than Muslim non-Arabs and generally did not mix with other Muslims," and "the new converts were not given the same rights as Muslim Arabs." The fiscal expression of this is the sharpest single fact in the card, because it shows the hierarchy being deliberately restored after being deliberately removed. The *jizya*, "which was traditionally reserved for the non-Muslim majorities of the caliphate, continued to be imposed on non-Arab converts to Islam" — that is, a man who accepted Islam kept paying the tax of an unbeliever if he was not an Arab. Umar II abolished it for converts; the same record states plainly that "Yazid II reversed Umar II's equalization reforms, reimposing the jizya on the mawali," which "sparked revolts in Khurasan in 721 or 722 that persisted for some twenty years." The historian G.R. Hawting is quoted in the same article for the summary judgment: "Islam was in fact regarded as the property of the conquering aristocracy." A related and stronger claim — that in the seventh and early eighth centuries "admission into the umma was reserved exclusively for Arabs" and a non-Arab convert "first had to gain membership in an Arab tribe by becoming the mawlā (client) of an Arab sponsor" — is recorded here as the attributed position of Dr. Michael Penn, not as this card's finding; it is contested in the field and this card does not certify it.

**The protest movement against that hierarchy took its name from the verse.** The Shu'ubiyya was "a social, cultural, literary, and political movement within the Muslim world that sought to oppose the privileged status of Arabs and the Arabization of non-Arab civilizations amidst the early Muslim conquests, particularly under the Umayyad Caliphate." Its name is derived, in the fetched article's own account, from *shu'ūb* — the word for "nations" or "peoples" in 49:13, which the article quotes in full at exactly that point. The archive should hold both halves of this without collapsing either: the verse was live enough in the civilization's mind to name a movement, and the movement existed because the verse was not being kept.

**Lineage was written into the law.** Two independent instances, both at the `fiqh` layer, both surviving into the classical consensus rather than being early aberrations. First, the caliphate: "Traditionally, Sunni Muslim madhhabs all agreed that a caliph must be a descendant of the Quraysh," a rule so entrenched that the Ottomans are described as having "defied the Hadiths that state that the Imam or Caliph has to be from the Quraysh tribe" when they claimed the office. The highest position in the community carried a hereditary tribal qualification. Second, marriage: under *kafa'ah*, the Shafi'i school "concerns the factors of lineage, religiousness, profession," with the fetched summary drawing the consequence explicitly — "an Arab woman should not marry a non-Arab man" — and the Hanbali school likewise counts lineage among its five factors. **The tradition also contained its own refusal of this**, and that belongs in the same paragraph: the Maliki position holds that kafa'ah is "the proportionality in religion for both husband and wife," and "in Shia Islam, there is no concept of kafa'ah on the basis of lineage." When Hadhrami sayyid stratification produced a public crisis in 1905, Rashid Rida and Ahmad Surkati argued against it from within the tradition and on the principle of equality. The doctrine was contested; it was also real, and it was law.

**The civilization's greatest historian argued that Black Africans were fit for slavery — after demolishing the theological version of the same idea.** This is the finding that makes the failure impossible to file as popular prejudice the scholars did not share. In the Muqaddimah, read directly this session in Franz Rosenthal's translation, Ibn Khaldun dismantles the Hamitic curse with real force: "Genealogists who had no knowledge of the true nature of things imagined that Negroes are the children of Ham, the son of Noah, and that they were singled out to be black as the result of Noah's curse… It is mentioned in the Torah that Noah cursed his son Ham. No reference is made there to blackness." He attributes skin colour to climate, and says the genealogists "were led into this error by their belief that the only reason for differences between nations is in their descent. This is not so." And then, in the chapter arguing that a nation subjugated by another loses its vitality — a chapter whose first worked example is the *Persians*, and which contains his own aside that "the rule of Islam is known for its justice" — he writes: "Therefore, the Negro nations are, as a rule, submissive to slavery, because (Negroes) have little (that is essentially) human and have attributes that are quite similar to those of dumb animals, as we have stated." Elsewhere he describes populations south of the Sudan as "humans who are closer to dumb animals than to rational beings… They cannot be considered human beings." He refuted a racism grounded in a curse and replaced it with a racism grounded in climate. The correction did not reach the conclusion.

**The prejudice was general enough to generate a literature answering it, and to pattern who was enslaved.** Al-Jahiz — himself, on the fetched account, the grandson of a Black *mawla* — wrote *Fakhr al-Sūdān 'alā al-Bīḍān*, "The Boast of the Blacks Over the Whites," and argued in *On the Zanj* that "the Zanj say that God did not make them black to disfigure them; rather it is their environment that made them so… There is no question of metamorphosis, or of punishment, disfigurement or favor meted out by Allah." A defence of that kind is evidence of the attack it answers. The fetched survey of the period states the two halves together in one sentence: "Though the Qur'an expresses no racial prejudice, ethnocentric prejudice towards black people is widely evident among medieval Arabs," and traces anti-Black racism to "racial hierarchies established during the trans-Saharan, Red Sea, and Indian Ocean slave trades." Ibn Khaldun's own text supplies the mechanism at ground level: of the Lamlam, "The people of Ghanah and Takrur invade their country, capture them, and sell them to merchants who transport them to the Maghrib. There, they constitute the ordinary mass of slaves." This is the ethnic patterning of slavery that **CMOS-0024** treats as its own subject; the two cards should be read together and neither softens the other.

**Scope-limiter.** This does **not** establish that the Qur'anic text licenses ethnic hierarchy; the text runs the other way, which is exactly why the record is legible as failure rather than as consistency. It does not establish that ethnic hierarchy was uniform across time or place — the same record contains Umar II abolishing the convert tax, Abu Hanifa's "the belief of a newly converted Turk is the same as that of an Arab from Hejaz," the Maliki and Shia refusal of lineage kafa'ah, and al-Jahiz's book, and this card does not resolve prevalence in either direction. It offers no comparative claim: nothing here says Muslim societies were better or worse on this axis than contemporaneous Byzantine, Latin Christian, Persian, Indian or Chinese societies, and the comparative question is not adjudicated by this evidence. It does not certify that conversion required Arab clientage; that is recorded as an attributed and contested position. It gives no figure for anything — no count of mawali taxed, no measure of how often kafa'ah was litigated on lineage, no estimate of the slave trades' volume. And it does not establish that the tradition failed to criticize itself: the Shu'ubi movement, the Maliki position, Rida and Surkati are all inside the tradition, and all of them are in this card.

3. **Ibn Khaldun's determinism was environmental, not biological essentialism in the modern sense**, and he explicitly rejected the Hamitic curse. That context is in the card because it is true. It does not soften the conclusion he reached, and this card does not let it.
4. **The "submissive to slavery" sentence sits inside a general theory of subjugation** whose first worked example is the Persians, not Black Africans. Quoting it without that frame would be a decontextualization; the frame is stated in the Claim. Neither does the frame rescue it — he applies to one people as a permanent racial disposition what he applies to another as a contingent political condition.
5. **Prevalence is not established, in either direction.** This card cannot say how many Muslims held these views, how often kafa'ah was litigated on lineage, or how the picture varied between al-Andalus, Khurasan, Egypt and the Deccan. Anyone using it to assert a civilization-wide constant is using it wrongly.
6. **No comparative claim is made or implied.** The card does not adjudicate whether Muslim societies were better or worse on ethnic hierarchy than contemporaneous Byzantine, Latin Christian, Persian, Indian or Chinese societies. That question needs its own card and its own evidence.
7. **The famous Farewell Sermon formula — "no superiority of an Arab over a non-Arab" — is deliberately not used in this card.** It is widely cited, and its canonical status and chain were not audited this session. The Bukhari- and Muslim-attributed reports quoted through Tafsir Ibn Kathir do the same work with sourcing this session actually performed.
8. **The valence label is contestable and the contest is recorded, not hidden.** A reasonable adjudicator could call this card `corrective` on the ground that its index trace was `quran` and its Qur'anic sublayer is the strongest thing in it. The Status section states why the evidence pushed the other way, and states the CMOS-0001 consistency objection rather than waiting for someone else to raise it. This is exactly the kind of judgment that should be re-examined at frontier sign-off.
9. **`synthesis` departs from the index's pre-carding layer estimate of `quran`.** The derivation is argued in Status. If a reviewer restores `quran`, the index trace must be corrected with it.

## Unsafe Wording to Avoid

> "Islam abolished racism."

> "Muslim societies never practiced racism or ethnic hierarchy." (This is the ledger's own recorded unsafe wording for this claim, and it is false.)

> "Racism is a Western invention that Muslims learned from Europe."

> "Because the Qur'an condemns tribalism, Muslim history is free of it."

> "Ibn Khaldun was simply a man of his time." (He was also the man who refuted the curse theory in the same book. The excuse does not fit its own subject.)

> "The Qurashi requirement was only political, not religious." (It is stated in the fetched record as the agreed position of the Sunni schools and grounded on hadith.)

> "The Shu'ubiyya proves Islam was anti-racist." (The Shu'ubiyya proves there was something to protest.)

Each line above is quoted so that it can be rejected, and each is rejected.

## Safe Wording

> Qur'an 49:13 recognizes peoples and tribes but makes righteousness — not lineage — the measure of nobility, and the classical exegetical tradition read it that way. Muslim societies then built ethnic hierarchy against that standard, and part of it entered the law: a Qurashi-descent qualification for the caliphate, lineage as a marriage-compatibility criterion in two of the four Sunni schools, a poll tax kept on non-Arab converts and restored after being abolished, and a slave trade patterned by ethnicity. Ibn Khaldun rejected the theory that Black Africans were cursed and still wrote that they were suited to slavery. The standard is genuine and the failure against it is genuine; both belong in any honest account, and the standard by which the failure is judged came from inside the tradition, which is why the tradition also produced the movements and reformers who fought it.

## Counterattack Simulation

### Counter: "You have just written the anti-Muslim polemicist's script for him, complete with citations, and called it an archive."

The alternative was to publish the verse and hide the Muqaddimah, which is what an archive does immediately before it stops being believed. The polemicist already has Ibn Khaldun; what he does not have is the Umar II abolition, the Maliki and Shia refusal of lineage kafa'ah, Abu Hanifa on the Turkish convert, al-Jahiz's book, and Rida and Surkati in 1915 — all of which are in this card because they are true, and none of which would be credible coming from a source that had suppressed the other half. The failure is stated first here precisely so that the rest cannot be read as damage control.

### Counter: "This is cherry-picking. You took two sentences out of a 1,300-page book and called it the civilization's verdict on race."

Two sentences from the Muqaddimah, plus a poll tax on converts restored by an ordinary act of state, plus a descent qualification the Sunni schools agreed on, plus a lineage criterion in two schools' marriage law, plus an entire literary movement that existed to protest Arab privilege, plus a genre of books written to defend Black people against prejudice. If it were cherry-picking, the cherries would not be in the law. And the card says explicitly that prevalence is not established and that no comparative claim is made — it convicts the tradition of holding a standard and failing it, not of being uniquely bad.

### Counter: "You are judging the 14th century by 21st-century standards. Everyone thought this way."

The card judges the 14th century by the 7th-century standard the 14th century had already received, quoted in full, and read that way by its own exegetes. That is the opposite of anachronism. And "everyone thought this way" is falsified inside this card by people who did not: Abu Hanifa, the Maliki jurists, al-Jahiz, the Shu'ubi writers, and Ibn Khaldun himself when he was demolishing the curse theory. The excuse requires an ignorance the sources do not show.

### Counter: "The Qurashi rule is not racism. Every polity has eligibility rules, and this one is about a specific tribe, not a race."

Accepted as a distinction and rejected as a defence. The verse does not say "lineage is not the measure except for tribal lineage"; the nouns it displaces are precisely *shu'ūb* and *qabā'il*, peoples **and tribes**. A hereditary qualification for the highest office is the exact category the text denies, whichever end of it you stand on. And the kafa'ah criterion is not tribal in that narrow sense: the fetched Shafi'i formulation is "an Arab woman should not marry a non-Arab man," which is an ethnic line, not a clan one.

### Counter: "So the archive concedes that Islam failed. Then why does it exist?"

Because the archive's product is its credibility, and a memory system that cannot record a failure is not a memory system. This card also refutes the accusation that Islam *taught* ethnic supremacy — with primary text, classical exegesis, and the etymology of the protest movement's own name — and that refutation is worth more coming from a source that just spent a thousand words documenting the failure. An archive that only exonerates has no evidentiary value when it exonerates.

### Counter: "You buried the good news. A reader finishes this thinking Islamic civilization was racist."

A reader finishes this knowing that the civilization held an explicit anti-lineage standard, produced the vocabulary of protest against its own violations, contained schools of law that refused lineage ranking outright, and generated reformers who broke lineage rules using its own principles — and also that it kept a tax on non-Arab Muslims, put descent in the qualification for its highest office, and produced the Muqaddimah's passages on Black Africans. If that reader concludes the record is mixed and the standard is real, they have understood the evidence. The card is not written to produce a feeling; it is written to produce that conclusion.
