## 1. Qur'an-first interpretive protocol (not Qur'an-only dogma)
**Order of inquiry, inexactness labeled:**
1. **Original Arabic and syntax:** exact consonantal/morphological reading, word roots, grammatical actor and object, limits, temporal markers, exceptions. `Quranic Arabic Corpus` https://corpus.quran.com/ for morphology; full Arabic edition and multiple translations for checks.
2. **Near context:** continuous passage (not cherry-picked verse before/after). What legal situation, actors, vows/treaties, narrative voice, referent and cessation conditions are explicit *in the Qur'an*? Keep translation brackets separate from actual Arabic.
3. **Whole-Qur'an network:** find relevant elsewhere verses, including those hard to reconcile; do not resolve genuine tension with a vague slogan. Differentiate baseline moral norms from particular war/legal injunctions.
4. **Qur'anic self-description:** claims about God, guidance, earlier revelations, messenger, `muhaymin`, `fitna`, `zulm`, `taqwa` and accountability. Keep philosophical premises separate from historical proof.
5. **Early external evidence:** dated non-Muslim witnesses, papyri, coins, inscriptions, early legal acts, geography; source chronology and author interest. Treat source criticism symmetrically.
6. **Early Muslim narratives/tafsir/hadith:** useful as **evidence for how a community understood a passage** and possible as extra-historical data, but establish their date, chain, internal differences and how/if they alter plain Quranic wording. Distinguish source from authority.
7. **Alternative serious readings:** classical majorities, early disagreements, modern defensive and expansionist models, Qur'an-primary/Qur'anist positions and informed outside criticism. Need explicit falsifiers for founder's preferred claim.
8. **Observed outcomes and comparison:** named wars, noncombatants harmed or spared, alternatives available, institutional benefit, challenges to ruler, accountability. Same centuries, regimes and constraints; apply to Muslims and others.
9. **Update with precise grading:** **[TEXT]**, **[ARABIC]**, **[TRADITION]**, **[HISTORY]**, **[HYPOTHESIS]**, **[CREATIVE]**, **[UNKNOWN]**. Not all are mutually exclusive; separate sentences.
10. **Return the strongest unanswered question.** New source/interpretation learned becomes a linkable delta, not an unverifiable theological "checkmate."

**Crucial Qur'an-first counterpressure:** Qur'an 4:59 urges obedience to God and the Messenger; 16:44, 59:7 also matter to traditional authority claims. A Qur'an-primary hermeneutic must **explain why** the scripture's references to the Messenger do/do not authorize particular later hadith corpora as binding. The Qur'an does not literally use later terms such as `Sahih al-Bukhari`, `fiqh madhhab` or `isnad grading`. **Qur'an-first ≠ “hadith are automatically fabricated”; traditions are evaluated as evidence/secondary authority according to explicit criteria.** Starting hypotheses about hadith “complexity/noise” should be tested against legal clarity/ambiguity and historical reliability; simplicity alone does not prove God intended any specific rule.

## 3. First detailed dossier: Qur'an warfare, how to read what is actually there
### A. The positive and restrictive network (not all 'pure pacifism')
- **22:39–40:** `أُذِنَ لِلَّذِينَ يُقَاتَلُونَ بِأَنَّهُمْ ظُلِمُوا` — permission for the wronged “who are being fought”; next verse those expelled for saying their Lord is God; **houses of worship of multiple communities** mentioned. https://quran.com/22/39-40
- **2:190:** `وَقَاتِلُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ الَّذِينَ يُقَاتِلُونَكُمْ وَلَا تَعْتَدُوا` — fight those fighting you; do not transgress. `qātilū` is Form III “fight”, not the same as `fa-qtulū` “kill” in 9:5. https://quran.com/2/190
- **4:75:** concern with oppressed men, women, children, and request for rescue; text is a call to fight on behalf of oppressed. https://quran.com/4/75 .
- **4:90:** if parties withdraw from fighting and offer peace, the verse says no authority to fight them. https://quran.com/4/90
- **8:61:** `وَإِن جَنَحُوا لِلسَّلْمِ فَاجْنَحْ لَهَا` — accept peace if enemy inclines to peace. https://quran.com/8/61
- **60:8–9:** God does not forbid kindness and justice toward those who did not fight you because of religion or expel you from home; differentiates actual hostility from otherness. https://quran.com/60/8-9
- **9:4:** maintain unbroken treaties with non-aggressor polytheists; https://quran.com/9/4 .
- **9:6:** `وَإِنْ أَحَدٌ مِّنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ اسْتَجَارَكَ فَأَجِرْهُ ... ثُمَّ أَبْلِغْهُ مَأْمَنَهُ` — if a polytheist seeks protection, grant asylum and escort to safety (including after hearing message). **Not a generic “convert or die” rule.** https://quran.com/9/6 .
- **9:13:** describes oath-breakers who attempted to expel Messenger and “attacked you first,” a direct intertext with 9:5-13; https://quran.com/9/13 .
- **47:4:** brutal battlefield language followed by binding prisoners and later releasing as grace or ransom until war ends. This is an important Quranic POW text but not a universal ban on captivity in later juristic practice; https://quran.com/47/4 .
- **2:256:** `لَا إِكْرَاهَ فِي الدِّينِ` — no compulsion in religion, but historical interpretive differences and minority-state hierarchy need separate research; https://quran.com/2/256 .
- **5:32:** contains saving/killing one vs all humanity in a decree specifically for **the Children of Israel**; scriptural continuity is the strongest story, not erasure of Jewish origin; https://quran.com/5/32 .
**Important boundary:** the Qur'an does **not explicitly enumerate** the later frequently quoted “don't kill women and children, don't cut trees, don't burn orchards” as one comprehensive list. Bukhari 3015 prohibition against killing women and children and reports attributed to Abu Bakr about trees/animal destruction belong to **hadith/early historical instructions**, and their transmission and scope must be evaluated. Quran 2:190's `lā taʿtadū` is broad, but *specific* rules cannot be said to be literal Quran verses without evidence.

### B. The hardest 9:5 in its actual movement
- **9:5** `فَاقْتُلُوا الْمُشْرِكِينَ حَيْثُ وَجَدتُّمُوهُمْ` — command to kill the polytheists in war and capture/besiege, followed by repentance/prayer/alms condition. This is **really severe**. https://quran.com/9/5
- **9:4** treaty-keeping polytheists are expressly **exempt**, with full-term pact enforcement. **9:6** orders protected asylum and safe escort for a polytheist who seeks it. **9:13** refers to opponents who broke oaths, conspired to expel the messenger and began fighting.
- **Reasonable narrow internal reading:** text addresses real treaty-breakers and hostile groups rather than an undifferentiated command to kill all non-Muslims; protection and safe passage are textual constraints, not imported apologetics.
- **Uncomfortable remainder:** 9:5 explicitly mentions repentance, prayer and alms as conditions for release in its own sentence. We should not explain that away as if no religious pressure is present; 9:6 supports possibility of nonconversion with safe escort, but whether the two cohere in every situation is an open question.
- **Classical competing doctrine:** some later exegetes/jurists read 9:5, alongside 9:29, as expanding/abrogating earlier fighting limits and permitting fighting broader classes. Source-close actual chronology/argument of *naskh*, early tafsir and legal applications; do not claim abrogation is explicitly Quranic instruction to repeal 2:190.
- **Next direct research:** syntactic scope of `al-mushrikīn` (definite article plus 9:1 treaty referents), who counts as `fa-qtulū` target; legal exceptions; chronology and early historical documentation. Form III `qātala` does not imply only a defensive initiator in every occurrence—the grammatical form by itself is not a moral verdict.

### C. The even harder 9:29 — where we must not manufacture context
The Arabic expressly orders fighting **`قَاتِلُوا الَّذِينَ ... مِنَ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ`** among People of the Book who meet stated religious criteria **until they pay `الْجِزْيَةَ`** `عَنْ يَدٍ وَهُمْ صَاغِرُونَ`. https://quran.com/9/29 .
- **What is explicit:** fighting, a named religious community, specified belief/legal characteristics, jizya, a relation conventionally translated as **subdued/humbled**. **Nothing in the isolated Arabic verse explicitly says “they had attacked first”**.
- **Classical mainstream/rival reading:** al-Tabari's expansive commentary names Jews and Christians more generally, ties the occasion to Tabuk, and discusses `sāghirūn` as subjection/abasement. Source Arabic/translated excerpts: https://tafseerquran.com/en/tafsir/9/29 ; https://quranponder.com/ponder/9/tafsir/al-tabari/25-29/ . The interpretation isn't automatically the Qur'an, but **it exists and historically mattered**.
- **Modern restricted reading:** Ahmed Al-Dawoody / other Muslim international-law scholarship argues immediate Byzantine threat and Tabuk context narrow the war. [Use of Force under Islamic Law](https://academic.oup.com/ejil/article/24/1/343/438602) sees 9:29 addressing a specific Byzantine border/political conflict rather than permanent warfare for disbelief. It is a responsible scholarly reading, **not the only one**. Also [Jihad and the Qur'an: Classical and Modern Interpretations in Oxford Handbook](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Shah-M-and-Abdel-Haleem-M-edd-%E2%80%93-The-Oxford-Handbook-of-Quranic-Studies-.pdf).
- **The debate over `min`:** read partitive (“some of”) vs explanatory (“from among/as for”) analysis in both classical and modern commentaries. Cannot choose solely by preference for a narrower rule.
- **The debate over `ʿan yad`, `sāghirūn`:** pay under political authority vs rituals of humiliation, and whether administration protected minorities while also subordinating them. Historical examples and qadi/fiscal papyri are necessary. “Jizya is just a neutral modern tax” conceals hierarchy; “jizya always meant torture and forced conversion” is equally unjustified.
- **Potential hard synthesis:** **9:5** has stronger treaty-breaker language in the immediately surrounding passage than **9:29** has explicit “aggression first” language. A Qur'an-first reader should *not* silently transfer 9:13's opponent identity to every People-of-the-Book referent in 9:29. Only further textual/structural evidence might justify it. These verses should be treated as **two distinct Qur'an-first cases**.
- **A real moral problem:** an authoritative criterion restricted to imminent attackers may explain context but not by itself answer how religious difference and tribute/subjection relate to 2:256 no compulsion, 3:113 praise of upright People of the Book or 60:8 justice to nonaggressors. Preserve the tension honestly.
- **Historical fact next:** Tabuk expedition accounts commonly trace an alleged Byzantine mobilization; whether a concrete invasion was actually imminent is independently debated and not directly established by Quran 9:29. Later caliphal wars into Byzantine/Sasanian territories cannot all be inferred from Muhammad's own Quranic war contexts; specific policy and material sources matter.
- **No false ending:** There is not yet a demonstrable proof from the whole Qur'an that 9:29 *must* universally command offensive conversion wars **or** that it *could only* apply to armed border aggressors. Rank alternatives by linguistic, contextual and historical evidence, and record where each explanation has costs.

### D. Cross-context balancing not *erasure*
**Other passages with real bite:** 8:39 `fitna` and religion for God; 2:193; 9:36; 48:16, 9:123; 47:4 (violent opening and release rule); 9:29. Read these, and traditional claims about abrogation, before publicizing “Qur'an only allows defense.” The best positive evidence for protection still matters; it doesn't cancel contradictory readings by rhetorical fiat.

## 4. The “religious corruption” golden thread: Scofield is a better example than it first appears
- **Founder's intuition:** religious elites/influencers can bend divine texts toward institutions/political projects. The **Scofield Reference Bible** illustrates how notes and headings guide meaning across generations.
- **Specific factual correction:** Scofield's Reference Bible was first published **1909**, revised **1917**. It placed **dispensationalist headings, marginal notes and cross-references alongside the preexisting King James Bible text**. This is **interpretive/parathextual influence**, not demonstrated alteration of the biblical base words. Oxford University Press's editorial history https://blog.oup.com/2017/04/scofield-reference-bible-centenary/ and edition records https://scripture.dbs.org/books/English-1901-Scofield-Reference-Study-Bible/ . It profoundly affected evangelical prophecy and Christian Zionist interpretations; distinguish theological influence from any unproven centralized text conspiracy.
- **Different corruption mechanisms:** (1) textual manuscript variant/interpolation; (2) translation choice; (3) commentary/footnotes or sectarian interpretation; (4) selection of canon; (5) administrative/clerical enforcement; (6) political misuse. A single “Bible was edited” claim conflates all six.
- **Qur'an 2:79, 3:78, 4:46, 5:13** must be read against **5:44–48 and 3:113**; Qur'an both recognizes earlier divine guidance and criticizes some communities' presentation/use of it. **Do not flatten all Jews or Christians into corrupt people; Quran itself distinguishes upright People of the Book (3:113).**
- **Historical Judaism:** Second Temple Jewish communities were diverse, and Jesus was a first-century Jew. The “Jesus reformed Judaism and Islam reformed Christianity” analogy is **theological and genealogical**, not permission to declare Judaism as a whole corrupted or dismiss Jewish interpretations. Christianity emerged within Jewish debates, and later theological formulations became distinct; early Islam arises in a plural late-antique religious context.
- **Most provocative fair question:** “When an institution prints an interpretation beside scripture for generations, how many readers can still tell the difference?” Apply symmetrically to Scofield, Quran translations with bracketed expansions and major tafsir, hadith canon, state-sponsored sermons and AI answers.

## 6. Source types and the “history written by victors” trap
- Early Arabic/Muslim narratives and much later historical compilations are vital but written and transmitted in particular intellectual/political worlds. Non-Muslim Syriac, Greek, Armenian and Coptic testimonies supply rivals; they too have biases and theological framing.
- Unlike “winners always wrote everything,” many textual records were preserved by conquered communities, monks, rival sects, imperial opponents, fiscal bureaucracies, travelers and inscriptions. Losing groups didn't necessarily vanish from memory.
- **Evidence hierarchy, not identity hierarchy:** dated contemporary records and inscriptions (but with genre limitations), securely attributable scriptural manuscripts, early external witnesses, later rich historical chronicles, retrospective hagiography, modern synthesis. Each is capable of revealing bias.
- **Original historical puzzle:** compare actual **seventh-century** Byzantine/Sasanian and Arabian warfare with contemporary religious rules, not later Vikings/Mongols as origin background. Later invasions can be separate stress tests for descendants of the moral operating system.
