# Load-Bearing Evidence and Narrative Mining OS

**Status:** Canonical research doctrine  
**Date:** 2026-07-25  
**Purpose:** Build a compounding system for discovering, verifying, connecting, and deploying the facts, records, questions, stories, mechanisms, and narrative structures that can change how people understand religion, history, power, morality, and civilization.

---

## 1. North Star

> **Do not merely collect information. Find the small number of truths that reorganize the rest of the map.**

A load-bearing item is not simply interesting. It carries disproportionate explanatory, moral, or narrative weight.

It may be:

- a fact that collapses a false narrative;
- a question that exposes a hidden assumption;
- a primary record that makes denial difficult;
- a story that gives a statistic a human face;
- a contradiction that reveals institutional capture;
- a mechanism that explains why the pattern keeps recurring;
- a comparison that corrects an asymmetric standard;
- a timeline that restores causality;
- an image, map, name, artifact, or quotation that makes history visible;
- a synthesis that connects previously isolated evidence into a new model.

The objective is not rhetorical victory at any cost. The objective is **truthful strength**: claims powerful enough to matter and disciplined enough to survive hostile scrutiny.

---

## 2. The Core Distinction

### Information
A datum that may be true but does not materially change the model.

### Evidence
Information that supports or weakens a specific claim.

### Load-bearing evidence
Evidence that materially changes the probability, meaning, or moral interpretation of a major claim.

### Hinge fact
A fact on which a broader narrative turns. Remove it and the story changes shape.

### Keystone case
A historical episode that concentrates a large system into one inspectable event.

### Narrative bridge
A fact, person, text, or story that allows one audience to understand another tradition without first accepting its entire worldview.

### Breaker evidence
Evidence that would disprove, sharply qualify, or force revision of our preferred thesis.

### Synthesis
A higher-order model that explains how multiple facts, incentives, institutions, and historical layers fit together.

### Consilience
Independent lines of evidence converging on the same conclusion.

> **The strongest public case is rarely one perfect fact. It is consilience: several independent trails meeting at the same truth.**

## 3. The Load-Bearing Test

Score every candidate from 0–5 on these dimensions:

1. **Truth confidence** — How strong is the source base?
2. **Consequence** — How much does it change the larger interpretation?
3. **Compression** — Can it be understood and repeated without distortion?
4. **Surprise** — Does it violate a common but false expectation?
5. **Causal relevance** — Does it reveal mechanism rather than coincidence?
6. **Defensibility** — Can it survive the strongest counterargument?
7. **Bridge value** — Can it reach people outside the existing audience?
8. **Human weight** — Does it restore moral reality, not merely abstraction?
9. **Visualizability** — Can it become a map, timeline, image, chart, scene, or artifact?
10. **Generativity** — Can it produce many honest questions, scripts, posts, lessons, or research branches?
11. **Uniqueness** — Does it add something not already supplied by stronger evidence?
12. **Misuse risk** — How easily can it become misleading, sectarian, triumphalist, or dehumanizing?

Prioritize candidates with high truth confidence, consequence, defensibility, bridge value, and generativity. Surprise without truth is clickbait. Emotion without context is manipulation. Data without mechanism is trivia.

## 5. Specialist Disciplines to Mine

Future research should deliberately invoke fields whose vocabulary exposes evidence ordinary searches miss:

- **Historiography:** how history is written and contested.
- **Source criticism:** origin, reliability, incentives, and context of sources.
- **Textual criticism:** reconstructing texts from manuscript variants.
- **Codicology:** manuscripts as physical objects.
- **Paleography:** historical handwriting and scripts.
- **Epigraphy:** inscriptions.
- **Numismatics:** coins and currency.
- **Diplomatics:** authentication and form of official documents.
- **Prosopography:** collective biography and elite/network reconstruction.
- **Microhistory:** a small case used to expose a large system.
- **Oral history:** recorded memory with explicit attention to memory limits.
- **Reception history:** how texts, events, and figures are interpreted over time.
- **Memory studies:** how societies remember, forget, ritualize, and weaponize history.
- **Historical sociology:** institutions, classes, states, and social change across time.
- **Cliometrics:** quantitative methods in history.
- **Historical demography:** populations, mortality, fertility, migration, and family structure.
- **Historical GIS:** spatial analysis of past events.
- **Social-network analysis:** relationships among rulers, scholars, financiers, institutions, and transmitters.
- **Comparative legal history:** how rules differ across systems and actual enforcement.
- **Causal inference:** separating correlation from mechanism.
- **Counterfactual history:** disciplined evaluation of plausible alternatives.
- **Longue durée:** slow structures operating beneath headline events.
- **Path dependence:** how early choices constrain later possibilities.
- **Genealogy:** tracing the historical construction of a concept or category.
- **Hermeneutics:** interpretation of texts and meaning.
- **Political theology:** how sacred concepts authorize political power.
- **Moral psychology:** conscience, obedience, identity, dehumanization, and self-justification.
- **Institutional economics:** incentives, governance, rents, and coordination.
- **Science and technology studies:** how technical systems embed political and moral choices.

## 6. The Mining Pipeline

### Stage 1 — Signal capture

Capture any:

- surprising fact;
- phrase;
- contradiction;
- source quotation;
- historical analogy;
- visual artifact;
- name;
- number;
- map;
- disputed claim;
- question;
- emotional reaction;
- intuition that “there is something here.”

Do not demand perfect articulation at intake. Seeds are often pre-verbal.

### Stage 2 — Claim decomposition

Break the seed into separate claims:

1. What happened?
2. Where and when?
3. Who decided?
4. What was intended?
5. What actually occurred?
6. Who was affected?
7. What sources establish each part?
8. What is inference rather than record?
9. What larger narrative does it challenge?
10. What would disprove the interpretation?

### Stage 3 — Source ladder

Climb in this order where possible:

1. primary text, record, artifact, or dataset;
2. official archive or institutional source;
3. serious scholarly synthesis;
4. specialist debate and counterargument;
5. reputable journalism;
6. public explainer;
7. social-media discovery lead only.

A viral post can identify a trail. It cannot terminate the trail.

### Stage 4 — Triangulation

Seek at least three independent kinds of support:

- documentary;
- quantitative;
- testimonial;
- material/spatial;
- scholarly contextualization;
- adversarial concession.

### Stage 5 — Adversarial audit

Ask:

- What is the strongest honest objection?
- Are we inferring motive from outcome?
- Are we confusing a district with a city, a sect with a religion, or an institution with a source?
- Are we comparing ideals asymmetrically?
- Does the fact remain meaningful after qualification?
- Is there a stronger formulation that is less sensational?
- What claim should we explicitly refuse to make?

### Stage 6 — Synthesis

Connect the case to:

- source vs. interpretation;
- blueprint vs. builder vs. building;
- incentives and institutional capture;
- moral visibility;
- power and accountability;
- recurring civilizational loops;
- comparative cases;
- modern implications.

### Stage 7 — Content atomization

From one verified case, produce:

- one canonical claim card;
- one source ledger;
- one timeline;
- one map or visual brief;
- five load-bearing questions;
- five compressed lines;
- one long-form essay;
- one short script;
- one carousel;
- one debate response;
- one comparative research branch;
- one breaker-evidence note.

### Stage 8 — Recursive memory

Record:

- what was verified;
- what failed;
- which wording survived scrutiny;
- which sources were strongest;
- which questions created breakthroughs;
- which narratives resonated without distortion;
- what future agents should mine next.

## 7. Standard Research Artifacts

The system should accumulate:

1. **Claim cards** — one defensible claim at a time.
2. **Proof stacks** — independent evidence converging on a thesis.
3. **Breaker ledgers** — disconfirming evidence and strongest objections.
4. **Chronology files** — sequence before interpretation.
5. **Actor maps** — interests, authority, funding, and relationships.
6. **Source hierarchies** — revelation, transmitted report, interpretation, law, institution, culture, state, propaganda.
7. **Quote vaults** — exact quotations with provenance and context.
8. **Adversarial concessions** — statements against the speaker’s apparent interest.
9. **Moral-witness files** — survivors, rescuers, dissenters, and repentant insiders.
10. **Contradiction pairs** — creed vs. conduct; label vs. fruit; rhetoric vs. record.
11. **Comparative matrices** — matched standards across traditions and civilizations.
12. **Maps and spatial overlays** — geography that changes interpretation.
13. **Visual artifact libraries** — documents, objects, buildings, inscriptions, photographs.
14. **Load-bearing question banks** — questions that expose causal and category errors.
15. **Narrative packs** — verified fact, context, caveat, visual, human story, and deployment options.
16. **Myth genealogies** — where a popular claim originated and how it spread.
17. **Omission ledgers** — important facts repeatedly excluded from standard narratives.
18. **Research queues** — ranked unknowns, not unstructured bookmarks.

## 8. The Source–Power–Fruit Stack

For religious and civilizational claims, separate at least these layers:

1. revelation or founding source;
2. founder/prophetic model;
3. early community;
4. transmitted reports;
5. interpretation and theology;
6. jurisprudence or doctrine;
7. institution;
8. political authority;
9. economy and material capacity;
10. culture and custom;
11. popular identity;
12. implementation;
13. historical fruit;
14. later memory and propaganda.

Never let a label collapse the stack.

> **A Christian state, Muslim empire, Jewish movement, secular republic, socialist government, or liberal institution is a claim of identity—not a self-authenticating description of conduct.**

## 9. High-Leverage Narrative Forms

### The Label–Fruit Test

> What did the institution call itself, and what did it repeatedly produce?

### The Founder–Institution Gap

> What did the founder teach, and what did the later institution reward?

### The Sacred Capture Story

> How did power borrow sacred language to legitimize interests the source itself may have restrained?

### The Unexpected Victim

A group identified with the aggressor’s own professed tradition becomes collateral to the machinery of power.

### The Unexpected Protector

A supposed outsider protects the vulnerable when insiders fail.

### The Reversal

The persecuted later becomes persecutor, or the reform movement becomes the institution it opposed.

### The Hidden Denominator

A dramatic number changes meaning once population, time, geography, or baseline is restored.

### The Map Epiphany

A spatial relationship makes an abstract event morally visible.

### The Timeline Epiphany

Sequence reveals that the popular cause came after the outcome it supposedly explains.

### The Adversarial Admission

An opponent’s own record supplies the strongest evidence.

### The Number With a Face

Scale plus biography prevents both denial and abstraction.

## 10. Load-Bearing Questions Library

- What exactly is being judged: source, interpretation, institution, state, culture, or actor?
- Is the label descriptive, aspirational, propagandistic, or legally defined?
- What would faithful implementation have required?
- What did the institution reward in practice?
- Who benefited materially from the sacred interpretation?
- Who bore the cost?
- What did insiders say privately?
- What is the earliest primary source?
- How close was the source to the event?
- What changed between the original source and later institution?
- Which part of the claim is fact, and which part is motive inference?
- What is the denominator?
- Compared with what historical baseline?
- Is this an exceptional case or a recurring pattern?
- What evidence would we expect if the thesis were false?
- What expected evidence is missing?
- Which counterexample most threatens our model?
- Does the claim survive its strongest qualification?
- Are we comparing scripture to conduct, or conduct to conduct?
- Are we comparing one tradition’s best with another’s worst?
- Did the moral language constrain power, legitimize power, or both at different times?
- Which institutions converted moral teaching into durable protection?
- What happened when enforcement became costly?
- What happened to people with no political leverage?
- Who preserved the memory, and who had reason to erase it?
- Why did this fact not become common knowledge?
- What image, place, object, or person makes the mechanism visible?
- What is the strongest honest one-sentence formulation?
- What seductive overstatement must we refuse?
- What new research branch does this case open?

## 11. Anti-Propaganda Guardrails

Do not:

- infer deliberate targeting of a religious group without evidence of intent;
- call a city “majority Christian” when the evidence supports only a Christian district or major community;
- treat all members of a faith as one historical actor;
- erase Christian abolitionists because Christian slaveholders existed;
- erase Muslim reformers because Muslim rulers committed abuses;
- use “not a real believer” as a universal escape hatch;
- use institutional crimes to automatically disprove the founding source;
- use a beautiful source to erase institutional crimes;
- present contested transmission as certainty;
- make “first ever” claims without precise scope;
- collapse centuries into one undifferentiated period;
- romanticize any golden age;
- sanitize victims to preserve a preferred civilizational story;
- weaponize suffering against descendants who did not commit it;
- confuse moral clarity with causal simplicity.

## 13. Master Mining Prompt

> Act as a truth-first meta-historian, investigative archivist, causal-inference researcher, comparative theologian, institutional analyst, and narrative strategist. Given the topic, identify every potentially load-bearing fact, statistic, ratio, timeline, primary document, legal record, quotation, testimony, artifact, map, contradiction, anomaly, adversarial concession, mechanism, incentive, source-transmission issue, historiographical dispute, moral witness, breaker fact, comparison, counterfactual, and narrative bridge. Separate record from inference; source from interpretation; religion from institution; institution from state; motive from outcome; and ideal from implementation. Rank candidates by truth confidence, consequence, defensibility, compression, bridge value, human weight, visualizability, and generativity. State the strongest honest formulation, strongest counterargument, necessary caveat, and overstatement to avoid. Then produce a claim card, proof stack, timeline, actor map, five load-bearing questions, five compression lines, a visual brief, and the next research branches.

## 14. Final Canon

> **Mine for truths that change the map, not trivia that decorates it. Preserve primary records, denominators, mechanisms, timelines, human stories, and breaker evidence together. Separate source from interpretation, label from fruit, and motive from outcome. Build consilience. Refuse exaggeration. Then compress the verified truth into forms that can travel without breaking.**
