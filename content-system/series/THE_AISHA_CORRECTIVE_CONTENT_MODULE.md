## Flagship Essay / Video

# The Woman Who Corrected Hadith

### Core argument

Muslims are often told that questioning a report about 'A'isha is disrespectful to tradition. Yet the tradition itself remembers 'A'isha correcting claims attributed to the Prophet when she believed narrators had misunderstood, omitted context, contradicted the Qur'an, or reported something inconsistent with what she personally knew.

Honoring her therefore cannot mean preserving only the reports that use her as evidence.

It must also mean restoring her authority to interrogate evidence.

## Content Sequence

### 1. The Age Debate Swallowed the Woman

Restore 'A'isha's adult life, scholarship, teaching, legal reasoning, and intellectual authority.

### 2. What Did 'A'isha Correct?

Build a source-hardened selection of her best-attested corrections of prominent transmitters and Companions.

### 3. A Strong Chain Does Not End Inquiry

Explain the difference between transmission reliability and complete historical or interpretive certainty.

### 4. The Qur'an as Criterion

Show how reports should remain beneath the Qur'an's governing architecture.

### 5. Biography Is Not Automatically Legislation

Introduce the normativity ladder:

> report → historical probability → contextual act → permission → general rule → recommendation → command → permanent state policy

Each ascent requires new evidence.

### 6. The Burden of Proof Rises With Coercion

A claim concerning private devotion cannot be treated identically to a claim authorizing power over another person's body, conscience, education, property, marriage, or life.

### 7. The Vulnerability Presumption

> **Where evidence is uncertain, power is unequal, and the weaker party bears irreversible harm, uncertainty must restrict the powerful—not expose the vulnerable.**

### 8. Guardian Supremacy Is the Deeper Problem

Explain why age floors fail when parental, judicial, clerical, or customary exceptions preserve adult control over the vulnerable person's will.

### 9. Do Not Replace Bukhari With a Modern Historian

Historical criticism must remain criticism, not a new personality cult. Confidence labels and adversarial review are mandatory.

### 10. Build Another 'A'isha

Ask what educational, financial, institutional, and cultural conditions would allow Muslim women to correct powerful male authorities today.

## Editorial Safeguards

1. Do not claim that every correction attributed to 'A'isha is equally historical.
2. Source each example independently.
3. Do not imply that isnad criticism has no value.
4. Do not portray Qur'an-first reasoning as permission for arbitrary rejection.
5. Do not claim a precise alternative age without adequate evidence.
6. Do not make one modern historian infallible.
7. Distinguish historical analysis from present protective policy.
8. Keep the vulnerable person's welfare visible in every legal discussion.
9. Avoid reducing 'A'isha to either victimhood or apologetic symbolism.
10. End with institution building, not merely argument.
