## 0. Decision and boundaries

The big public asset is **comparative historical visibility**, not a civilization ranking. A visitor should see two/three dated places and comparable objects at the same time and walk away understanding **what existed, how it worked, why it developed, and what remains unknown**. We are not generating an "Islam > Christianity" scoreboard. The documented existence of sophisticated Byzantine or Latin Christian institutions makes a juxtaposition *stronger*, not weaker.

This is a **research** artifact. The production system is Truth & Peace Engine (T&P) and the public brand remains the standalone **Islamic Golden Ages** universe. Cross-post genuinely bridge-building episodes selectively to **Islam Loves Jesus**. Do not create a parallel claims database: resolve every atomic factual claim through existing CMOS ledger/cards and established gates. ## 1. Comparison standard: same century, same question, honest asymmetry

Before building a comparison card, register the following:

1. **Temporal match:** bounded years and century in each cell, not one medieval case against the 21st century. Later/earlier contrasts must be labeled explicitly as *transmission* or *before/after*, not simultaneous.
2. **Place and unit:** city, polity, community, institution, text, or particular social stratum. "Islam" vs "Europe" is invalid analytical grain.
3. **Comparable dimension:** e.g., hospital staffing, treatment, financing, charitable duty, animal-care norm, book production, translation network, women's patronage. Do not compare normative teaching on one side to practice on the other.
4. **Evidence stack:** primary period sources where possible → material/archival records → independent specialist scholarship. Quote/date/translation and later retrospective claims are separately tagged. Publication-safe wording requires existing gate, not the writer's confidence.
5. **Symmetric inquiry:** identify an actual institution/achievement for every side, not a staged weak opponent. Record each side's counterevidence and failures. If a comparator's evidence is missing, mark **unknown**, never "did not exist."
6. **What are we measuring?** A surviving regulation is not a patient outcome; a hadith is not proof of universal practice; a building is not proof of continuous free care; famous manuscripts are not evidence that all residents read.
7. **Who benefited?** Class, sect, gender, patronage, minority status, and geography materially change conclusions. Distinguish an ideal from a rule, funding, actual access, and enforcement.
8. **Transfer and mixing:** make routes explicit when supported (Greek → Syriac → Arabic; Arabic → Latin; Byzantine / Persian / South Asian contributions). Avoid civilizational ownership myths.
9. **Don't abuse absence:** catalog bias, archival survival, translation gaps, difference in record genres, and periodization can create false "firsts."
10. **Audience-facing answer:** name what is surprising, include one visible artifact/date, articulate the narrow established conclusion, preserve one honest limit. Never infer one theology is true or all adherents behave alike from a case.

### Comparison card schema — attach to existing claim system, do not fork it

`id | audience question | same-century cells (date/place/item) | comparator axis | claimant claim IDs | counterevidence IDs | primary/secondary source URIs | status (unverified/research-gated/publish-cleared) | safe public wording | unallowed overclaim | source/visual credits | owner | T&P destination`.

**A comparison becomes publish-cleared ONLY when underlying claims have been adjudicated in CMOS under its standing protocol.** “Sources attached” is not “closed.”

## 2. Pilot cards: factual starting points and explicit gaps

### COMP-01 — Three ways to build a hospital, 12th century

**Question / hook:** "In the 1100s, what did a hospital look like in Constantinople, Damascus, and London?"

Three **simultaneous medieval** exemplars, not an invented duel:

- **Constantinople — Pantokrator foundation, typikon October 1136**: detailed foundation document covering a hospital and aged care. Surviving *prescriptive* record; its promised staffing/beds and evidence of actual delivered care must not be conflated.
- **London — St Bartholomew's Hospital, founded 1123**: historically attested religious charitable foundation. The hospital's later modern staffing and subsequent transformations must not be back-projected into 1123.
- **Damascus — Nur al-Din Bimaristan, AH 549 / 1154 CE**: documented hospital foundation; monument and accounts provide an entry point to care, training, architectural form and later operation. Do not presume every class of patient was treated free without records.

**Comparison axes:** patron and legal form; funding; formal staffing; treatment vs hospice function; teaching; access; building versus documented operations; archival survival.

**Solid lead sources (verification by CMOS still required):**
- Official St Bartholomew's NHS hospital history: https://www.bartshealth.nhs.uk/st-bartholomews-our-history/
- Museum With No Frontiers monument record (Damascus): https://islamicart.museumwnf.org/database_item.php?id=monument%3BISL%3Bsy%3BMon01%3B15%3Ben
- Pantokrator foundation overview: https://pantokrator.gr/en/monastery-of-pantokrator/pantocrator/pantocrator-monastery-constantinople
- Scholarly edition: John Thomas and Angela Constantinides Hero (eds.), *Byzantine Monastic Foundation Documents*, Dumbarton Oaks, Pantokrator typikon no. 28 (Jordan translation, Gautier edition)
- Comparative scholarship: William R. Jones, "The Clinic in Three Medieval Societies," *Diogenes* 31(122), 1983, pp. 86–101: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/diogenes/article/abs/clinic-in-three-medieval-societies/4598337170EE67656AB096ABEFE2DC8B

**Safe initial description:** "In the twelfth century, Christian and Muslim societies established different kinds of charitable medical institutions. Their funding, staffing, intended beneficiaries and surviving evidence varied."  
**Unsafe:** "Muslims invented hospitals while Europe had none"; "each institution offered free modern medicine"; "we know which gave the best care."  
**Gate-needed:** three defensible institution-specific descriptions, archival-status labels, a specialist comparison of organizational forms, any public claims about treatment or access.

### COMP-02 — Who made the map? Palermo, 1154

**Question / hook:** "The Muslim geographer. The Christian king. One world map."

- **Palermo / Norman Sicily — al-Idrisi completed a geographic project for King Roger II, 1154.** The event itself challenges a picture of sealed civilizations; this is a collaboration/transfer comparison rather than a winner–loser contest.
- **Comparators to research:** contemporary cartographic traditions across the Mediterranean and Song China, with actual artifacts and maps rather than a generic "what Europe thought."
- **Compare:** source inputs, patronage, accuracy standards, manuscript survival, transmission.

**Lead:** Yale Iberian Connections curated summary: https://iberian-connections.yale.edu/resources/al-idrisis-map-1154/  
**Caveat:** Later composite reconstructions of the map must be labeled as reconstructions; don't claim the map depicted every region accurately or that its patron and maker had no political constraints. Yale's text has a Roger-number phrasing that must be checked against Roger II's death and conventional cataloging before copy is source-closed.

### COMP-03 — Who kept knowledge moving? Translation networks, 8th–11th centuries

**Question / hook:** "The history of science does not have a single language."

- **Abbasid-era Arabic scholarship:** Greek, Syriac, Pahlavi and Sanskrit material moved through translation networks with multiple patrons and scholars of different religious backgrounds.
- **Comparators:** Byzantine text production, Latin scholarly networks, Indian mathematical antecedents, Tang/Song knowledge traditions; pick specific city/century and object per episode.
- **Compare:** translator network, manuscript patronage, technical innovation, what was added, transmission routes, missing links.

**Lead:** Scott L. Montgomery, "Mobilities of Science: The Era of Translation into Arabic," *Isis* 109 (2018): https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/698236  
**Caveat:** "Preserved everything"; "the entire modern world was invented in Baghdad"; and a fictional single House of Wisdom lab are unsafe. Translation, criticism, synthesis, and cross-confessional talent are real topics requiring particular cases.

### COMP-04 — Mercy to animals: text, duty, institution, result

**Question / hook:** "A thirsty dog. An imprisoned cat. What did a civilization believe people owed animals?"

- **Early Islamic Qur'anic layer:** Qur'an 6:38 refers to animals and birds as communities. https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=6&verse=38
- **Hadith reception layer:** Bukhari 2363 (thirsty dog and forgiveness); Bukhari 3482 and/or 2365 (cat confined and starved). https://sunnah.com/bukhari/42/11 and https://sunnah.com/bukhari/60/149
- **Later Islamic historical layer:** investigate independently the actual fiqh, waqf deeds, shelters, veterinary interventions, and economic treatment of exhausted working animals. Existing CMOS animal ledger marks many broad institutional claims unresolved.
- **Contemporaries to compare later:** specific medieval Christian, Jain, Buddhist, Jewish and other animal-ethics texts and practices, with bounded dates, legal duties and enforcement; *not* an unsourced claim that others lacked compassion.

**Research ladder:** scriptural norm → transmitted account → legal duty → funded institution → observably improved treatment → post-use obligation.  
**Unsafe:** "Islam invented animal rights"; "the world's first free animal hospital"; "all premodern Muslim animals were protected"; "this hadith occurred during the Islamic Golden Age."  
**High-interest next question:** Did any real, durable endowment care for animals *after* they stopped being economically useful? Require deed, beneficiary class, revenue and operating record.

### COMP-05 — Mary/Jesus, shared reverence and real theological differences

**Question / hook:** "What do Christians and Muslims both say about Mary? Where do the accounts diverge?"

- **Primary comparison:** Qur'an 19:16–36 and Luke 1:26–38, each accurately translated and quoted at limited length. Compare annunciation, moral significance, miraculous birth, and distinct Christological interpretation.
- **Cross-historical bridge:** actual Christian scholars producing Arabic-language medical, philosophical and translation work during Abbasid periods. Don't suggest doctrinal agreement because scholars collaborated.
- **Scholarly lead:** Neuwirth, *The Qur'an and Late Antiquity* (2019); Christian contributions to Abbasid medicine (Worthing, 2024): https://reference-global.com/doi/10.2478/acm-2024-0015

**Safe description:** "Islam and Christianity both honor Mary and Jesus, but understand their identities and roles differently."  
**Unsafe:** "The two religions teach exactly the same thing about Jesus" or any account that erases doctrinal differences.

Source-specific, dated comparison of **Maragha Observatory (1259 and later)** with contemporary other sites; then Samarkand (15th century) in its own correctly dated episode. ## 4. Public editorial mechanics for T&P handoff

**A comparison headline is a question, not a verdict.** Recommended formats:

- **Time-locked triptych:** same century, three cities, named objects, two factual axes, an unresolved question.
- **Before / during / after:** evidence-backed transmission *within the same knowledge tradition* across time, with named sources and no implied exclusivity.
- **Moral teaching vs lived history:** quotations as attributed text on one panel; verified implementation on another; gap plainly stated.
- **Unexpected collaboration:** one actual creator, one actual patron, one traceable piece of work.
- **The failure test:** a beautiful principle confronted with a documented instance where power violated it.

**Five-second comprehension goal:** A cold viewer can say *when, where, what was compared, one surprise, and the limitation*. No dense false precision, cherry-picked scorecards, nation-vs-civilization diagrams, or "winner" trophies.
