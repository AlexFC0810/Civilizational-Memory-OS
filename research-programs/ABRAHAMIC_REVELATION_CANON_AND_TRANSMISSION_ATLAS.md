**Purpose:** Reconstruct how revelation, proclamation, scripture, manuscript, canon, interpretation, institution, and communal memory relate across Judaism, Christianity, and Islam without flattening their radically different source structures.

---

# Governing thesis

> **“Scripture” is not one historical object repeated three times. Torah, Gospel, Qur’an, canon, commentary, liturgy, law, and communal memory formed through different transmission architectures and must be studied on their own terms before comparison.**

The atlas exists to prevent five recurring collapses:

1. revelation into surviving manuscript;
2. original proclamation into later canon;
3. textual variation into total corruption;
4. canon formation into deliberate conspiracy;
5. inherited interpretation into the text itself.

It also prevents the opposite apologetic error:

> the existence of ancient manuscripts does not prove that every wording, interpretation, canon boundary, or theological construction is identical to the originating revelation.

The research task is not to declare continuity or corruption in advance.

It is to identify what was revealed, proclaimed, written, transmitted, selected, interpreted, institutionalized, contested, preserved, altered, concealed, translated, and recovered—and with what degree of confidence.

# The object stack

Every claim must identify its actual object.

## 1. Claimed revelation

What divine communication is the tradition claiming occurred?

Examples:

- revelation to Moses;
- prophetic instruction within Israel;
- the Injil associated with Jesus in the Qur’an;
- the teaching, life, and mission of Jesus in Christian memory;
- Qur’anic revelation to Muhammad ﷺ.

## 2. Prophetic proclamation and embodiment

What did the prophet publicly teach, perform, prioritize, prohibit, forgive, command, and model?

## 3. Earliest recoverable witness

What is the earliest evidence now available?

This may include:

- inscriptions;
- manuscript fragments;
- quotations;
- liturgy;
- communal practice;
- legal memory;
- correspondence;
- archaeological evidence;
- hostile or outsider testimony.

## 4. Textual tradition

How did wording travel?

Map:

- oral recitation;
- scribal copying;
- codices;
- textual families;
- variants;
- harmonization;
- translation;
- standardization;
- loss;
- recovery.

## 5. Canon

Which writings were recognized as authoritative, by whom, where, and when?

Canon is not identical to composition.

A text may exist before its boundaries or authority are settled.

## 6. Interpretation

What did communities believe the text meant?

Separate:

- lexical meaning;
- literary context;
- legal interpretation;
- theological interpretation;
- liturgical use;
- allegory;
- polemic;
- later dogmatic synthesis.

## 7. Institution

Which institutions preserved, taught, enforced, translated, censored, or privileged the text?

## 8. Political incorporation

How did kingdoms, empires, courts, colonial states, or modern nations shape the canon’s use and public meaning?

## 9. Lived religion

What did ordinary communities actually know, recite, hear, practice, or inherit?

## 10. Modern reconstruction

What do textual criticism, manuscript studies, archaeology, philology, historical criticism, and digital collation allow scholars to infer today?

No later layer should be silently projected backward into an earlier one.

# Qur’anic claims about previous revelation

The atlas must classify rather than compress the Qur’an’s discourse.

Research categories:

## Affirmation

Passages affirming that God revealed guidance to earlier prophets and communities.

## Confirmation

Passages describing the Qur’an as confirming what came before it.

## Criterion and guardianship

Passages presenting the Qur’an as a criterion, witness, or guardian over previous revelation.

## Distortion

Claims involving alteration, displacement, twisting, or changing words from their places.

## Concealment

Claims that people hid or withheld part of what they knew.

## Forgetting or loss

Claims that communities forgot or abandoned a portion of what they were reminded of.

## Misinterpretation

Claims involving tongues, readings, or assertions presented as scripture when they were not from God.

## Selective obedience

Theological corruption may occur through disobedience even where words remain present.

The project must not assume that every Qur’anic criticism describes the same mechanism.

> **Textual alteration, interpretive distortion, concealment, selective quotation, canon formation, translation, and practical betrayal are distinct hypotheses.**

# The Injil problem

The term must be studied before it is translated into a later category.

Competing objects to test:

1. a direct revelation granted to Jesus;
2. Jesus’ divinely authorized teaching and proclamation;
3. the salvific good news associated with his mission;
4. an oral or textual body known to his followers;
5. the Christian Gospel tradition available in Late Antiquity;
6. the four canonical Gospels collectively;
7. a Qur’anic category not reducible to any single surviving book.

Required evidence:

- Qur’anic usage;
- late antique Syriac, Greek, Arabic, Ethiopic, and other relevant terminology;
- Christian self-description;
- earliest Islamic exegesis;
- manuscript and canon history;
- evidence of Christian communities in and around Arabia;
- modern specialist disagreement.

Truth boundary:

> Do not claim that the Qur’anic Injil is straightforwardly identical to the modern New Testament. Do not claim that it must have been a lost bound book unless evidence supports that model.

- oral preaching and storytelling;
- lectionaries;
- translated fragments;
- inscriptions;
- monasteries and trade routes;
- apocryphal and para-biblical traditions;

The map must distinguish:

- existence somewhere in Late Antiquity;
- existence near Arabia;
- availability in Arabic;

Similarity is not proof of copying.

Absence of a surviving Arabic manuscript is not proof that no translated or oral material circulated.

# Continuity matrix

Do not ask only whether a text was “preserved.” Ask what kind of continuity is in view.

## Verbal continuity

Are the words substantially stable?

## Semantic continuity

Does the meaning remain stable despite wording or translation differences?

## Narrative continuity

Does the same event or teaching remain identifiable?

## Ritual continuity

Was the passage or teaching continuously recited or enacted?

## Legal continuity

Did communities preserve its normative function?

## Theological continuity

Did later doctrine preserve, extend, narrow, or reverse the earlier claim?

## Moral continuity

Did the tradition preserve the prophetic priority and fruit?

A text can possess high verbal continuity and contested interpretation.

A teaching can possess semantic continuity across different wording.

A community can preserve a passage while betraying it institutionally.

# Corruption taxonomy

The word “corruption” is too imprecise for research use unless typed.

Use:

- **C1 — Scribal alteration**
- **C2 — Textual loss**
- **C3 — Textual addition**
- **C4 — Harmonization**
- **C5 — Translation drift**
- **C6 — Canon selection or exclusion**
- **C7 — Interpretive distortion**
- **C8 — Concealment or selective quotation**
- **C9 — Institutional monopolization**
- **C10 — Practical betrayal**
- **C11 — Polemical misrepresentation**
- **C12 — Modern ideological projection**

Multiple types may coexist.

A claim of C7 does not prove C1.

A claim of C6 does not by itself prove conspiracy.

A claim of C10 may coexist with strong textual preservation.

# Public compression

Approved:

> **The Qur’an does not treat earlier revelation as worthless. It affirms that God revealed guidance before it, while also accusing communities of concealment, distortion, forgetting, misreading, and disobedience. Those are not all the same claim, so the historical question must be investigated layer by layer.**

Approved:

> **The Gospel in Christian history can mean Jesus’ good news, the proclamation about him, individual written Gospels, or the canonical collection. The Qur’anic Injil must be studied in that late-antique semantic world, not simply equated with a modern Bible without argument.**

Rejected:

> “The Bible is completely corrupted.”

Rejected:

> “The Qur’an confirms every sentence of the modern Bible exactly as it stands.”

---

# Final research doctrine

> **Name the object. Date the witness. Map the transmission. Separate wording from meaning, canon from composition, interpretation from revelation, and institution from prophet. Then state only what the evidence can carry.**

The aim is not to dissolve faith into manuscripts.

It is to prevent manuscripts, institutions, and inherited arguments from being mistaken for the whole history of revelation.
