## Why this record exists

This conversation developed a recurring founder concern into a potentially important research and public-education lane:

> Religious truth claims should not be evaluated only by creeds, apologetics, labels, or institutional power. They should also be tested by the moral fruit they produce when a hungry, homeless, endangered, hated, or politically inconvenient person appears.

The immediate trigger was a user-supplied testimony from a formerly homeless man in Pickens County, Georgia, who said Muslims first offered him work, two local people helped put a roof over his head, and Latino community members later gave him employment, while he experienced rejection from local white businesses and a self-identified Christian community.

The testimony is emotionally and morally important. It is not, by itself, a representative survey of Christians, white residents, Muslims, Latinos, Pickens County, or religious institutions. The speaker’s general claims remain testimony unless independently verified.

## Founder voice — central observations

The founder distinguishes sharply between:

This is a theological interpretation and should be labeled as such when presented in comparative or historical work.

## The strongest conceptual distinction

The emerging issue is not best framed as:

> Christianity versus Islam.

It is better framed as:

> **Prophetic religion versus tribal religion.**

Prophetic religion places the believer, ruler, nation, institution, and in-group under moral judgment.

Tribal religion reverses the relationship: it recruits God, scripture, clergy, and sacred identity to protect the in-group from judgment.

### Prophetic religion asks

- Is the hungry person fed?
- Is the stranger welcomed?
- Is the vulnerable protected?
- Is the enemy still treated justly?
- Does truth remain binding when it implicates one’s own side?
- Can rulers, armies, markets, and religious authorities be condemned?
- Does repentance produce changed conduct and repair?

### Tribal religion asks

- Is the person one of us?
- Did they affirm the approved creed or identity?
- Does helping them threaten our political coalition?
- Can wrongdoing by our side be reclassified, ignored, spiritualized, or blamed on the victim?
- Can grace, chosenness, prophecy, or security be converted into immunity?

## Christian theological correction — avoid a straw man

The concern should not be expressed as though Christianity, in its classical forms, simply teaches that conduct does not matter.

Christian scripture contains severe internal tests of fruit and action:

- Matthew 25 links treatment of the hungry, thirsty, stranger, unclothed, sick, and imprisoned to treatment of Christ;
- James 2 says faith without works is dead;
- the Good Samaritan places neighbor-love above tribal and religious boundary;
- 1 John 3 rejects love that remains words while material need is ignored;
- Matthew 7 warns that trees are known by their fruit;
- Romans 6 rejects the idea that grace licenses continued wrongdoing;
- Ephesians 2 joins salvation by grace to a life created for good works.

Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant traditions formulate faith, grace, works, sacraments, sanctification, and salvation differently, but mainstream Christianity generally does not teach that deliberate cruelty is morally irrelevant.

More precise diagnoses include:

- **cheap grace** — forgiveness detached from discipleship, repentance, sacrifice, and transformation;
- **cultural Christianity** — Christianity functioning as identity, ancestry, nation, or social tribe more than lived obedience;
- **creed detached from fruit**;
- **Christian nationalism** — the fusion of Christian symbols and national power;
- **Christian Zionism** — specific theological systems that may identify support for modern Israeli state policy with biblical fidelity;
- **institutional capture** — religious language serving political, racial, military, or economic interests.

The research lane should criticize failures through Christianity’s own highest standards, not merely from outside it.

## Islam and institutionalized mercy

The founder’s attraction to Islam is partly practical and observable: Muslims sometimes appear to live recognizable elements of Jesus’s ethic through hospitality, charity, sobriety, prayer, modesty, community, and concrete aid.

Relevant Islamic mechanisms include:

- **zakat** — obligatory redistribution;
- **sadaqah** — voluntary charity;
- **waqf** — durable charitable endowment;
- **ummah** — belonging that can cross ethnicity and nationality;
- fasting as embodied solidarity and discipline;
- repeated Qur’anic linkage of belief, prayer, justice, feeding, and material responsibility;
- accountability before God for concealed as well as public conduct.

The first word traditionally identified in the earliest Qur’anic revelation is **iqraʾ** — “Read/Recite.” It is more precise to call this the first word of revelation in Islamic tradition than the first command in the Qur’an’s compiled order.

Islam must also remain subject to the Fruit Test. Muslim identity, institutions, states, clerics, and communities can reproduce racism, nationalism, cruelty, hypocrisy, indifference, authoritarianism, and class hierarchy. The purpose of comparison is not to replace Christian triumphalism with Muslim triumphalism.

## Baby-formula social experiment — current status

The likely source is TikTok creator Nikalie Monroe’s late-2025 “testing your church” series.

Public reporting indicates:

- she called dozens of churches and other religious institutions while posing as a mother whose infant needed formula;
- the Islamic Center of Charlotte immediately offered assistance and asked what formula was needed;
- multiple Christian churches refused, delayed, redirected, or failed to give useful information;
- several Christian churches, including Catholic, Black, Hispanic, and conservative congregations, did offer help;
- at least one small Kentucky church’s helpful response generated major public donations;
- the exercise was informal, non-controlled, used deception, did not use a perfectly standardized script, and should not be represented as a scientific comparison of religions.

Its proper use is as an institutional-design case:

- Can front-line staff recognize an emergency?
- Is there discretionary money or a rapid response path?
- Are referral systems accurate and immediate?
- Does fear of scams eliminate mercy?
- Can a large benevolence bureaucracy fail at the exact moment aid is needed?
- Do institutions learn after public failure?

## The Qur’an as reformation and clarification

## Strategic direction — do not build an anti-Christian channel

The Stockfish move is not to tell Christians that Muslims are morally superior.

It is to invite Christians into a test authored by Jesus:

- feed the hungry;
- welcome the stranger;
- visit the prisoner;
- love the enemy;
- tell the truth;
- refuse hypocrisy;
- judge the tree by its fruit;
- place God above empire.

This is harder to dismiss as outside attack and more likely to activate sincere Christians as allies.

At the same time, invite Muslims into the Qur’an’s own tests:

- justice against self and kin;
- generosity beyond display;
- defense without transgression;
- care for orphan, poor, traveler, captive, and animal;
- no racial superiority;
- no compulsion;
- no concealment of testimony;
- repentance demonstrated through repair.

The project should become a reciprocal mirror, not a tribal scoreboard.
