## Chat-mine BEFORE generating (lever 4)
1. "What are the 3 most counterintuitive, quotable lines in these sources?"
2. "Find the exact passage where the strongest caveat is stated."
3. "If Islam was "spread by the sword," why were fewer than one in ten of the conquered — in Iran, Iraq, Syria, Egypt, Tunisia, and Spain — Muslim a full century after the armies arrived? And why is the land with the largest Muslim population on earth today, Indonesia, one that no Muslim army ever conquered — reached by merchants and mystics, not cavalry? But if political power played no part, why did conversion track the jizya, the cities, and access to the ruling class — and why did it come, in some times and places, at the point of a sword after all?"
4. "If Islam was "imposed by force from day one," account for the Prophet's own first act of government at Medina. It was not a battle. It was a written, negotiated pact that made the town's Muslims and its Jewish clans one community for mutual defense — "for the Jews their religion, and for the Muslims theirs" — with disputes referred to arbitration. Even the most skeptical modern historians of early Islam accept that such a founding compact existed. What kind of "imposition by force" opens by writing the non-Muslims' religious freedom into the founding charter of the state?"
5. "When you picture "Islamic law," you picture a state enforcing a single code. But for most of a thousand years, who actually decided what the law said — the caliph, or private scholars in competing schools that the caliph appointed judges to obey and could not simply overrule? And if the law was never stored in the palace, what happened to it when the palace fell?"
6. "If Islamic civilization was insular and hostile to other faiths' knowledge, why was the caliphs' chief translator a Nestorian Christian, their physicians a Christian dynasty six generations deep, their Baghdad court astronomer a Sabian who never converted, and Saladin's own doctor a Jew — and why, in the same breath, did that system tax and legally subordinate those very men as dhimmis?"
7. "If Islam were pure triumphalism — "we are God's favored, and our success proves it" — why does its own scripture keep a running catalogue of the richest and mightiest civilizations of the past, insisting they were stronger than you, and holding them up as warnings? What kind of holy book makes the grandest empires its case studies in ruin, names the palace-builders and pillar-raisers in the indictment, and tells you to go travel the land and look at the wreckage?"
